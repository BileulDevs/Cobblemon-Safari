# Cobblemon Safari: technical notes

How the mod works under the hood, for contributors, datapack makers and server owners who want the details. The player-facing presentation is in [README.md](README.md); every config key is explained in [CONFIG.md](CONFIG.md).

Every item of the mod (ticket, Escape Rope, gate, wall screen, decor) is in its own creative tab, "Cobblemon Safari" (`item/SafariCreativeTab`, registered by each loader: `FabricItemGroup` on Fabric, the creative tab registry event on NeoForge).

## Layout

| Module | Role |
|---|---|
| `common` | All gameplay logic, payloads, HUD, worldgen data |
| `fabric` / `neoforge` | Thin bridges from loader events to `SafariServerEvents` |
| `tools/worldgen` | Generator of every worldgen JSON file (do not hand-edit its output) |
| `tools/advancements` | Generator of the Safari advancement tab and its translations (do not hand-edit its output) |
| `example` | One example of everything that can be customized: config files (with a reference of every key), a datapack (spawns, NPCs, dialogue, gates, ruins) and a resource pack (texts, textures, sounds); see `example/README.md` |

## Worldgen

The Safari is a single island (shape radius 768 blocks, land to about 660, centred on 0,0) in an endless turquoise sea, enclosed by its own square world border. A small summit sits at the centre, ringed by rocky hills and a volcano. Around it, 14 habitats are laid out as sectors from the rocky ring to the coast, each with a whole sector of its own: grassland (north, where players arrive), savanna, dunes, badlands, jungle, marsh, mushroom grove, flower meadow, woodland, deep forest, taiga, snowfield, glacier, rocky hills.

The jungle mixes vanilla's jungle, sparse jungle and bamboo jungle in one biome: its trees are four placed features of our own (`placed_feature/safari/jungle_*`, from `jungle_placed_features` in the generator, in place of vanilla's `trees_jungle` through the biome's `replace_features`), counted by vanilla's `noise_threshold_count` on the biome-info noise: thick jungle where it is above `JUNGLE_DENSE_NOISE`, bamboo and its trees below `JUNGLE_BAMBOO_NOISE`, sparse jungle trees everywhere, so most of the biome is open jungle between patches about a hundred blocks across. That noise has a fixed seed in vanilla, so the patches do not move with the map's seed.

The terrain is kept fairly even from the centre to the coast, so large builds have room: continentalness and erosion follow smooth radial profiles (`CONTINENT_PROFILE`, `EROSION_PROFILE`, through `safari/island_radial`) with only a little noise on top. The summit's peaks are limited to the inner tenth of the radius; the rest of the island is plains and rolling hills (about 65 to 120 blocks high), dropping to the sea over the last eighth of the radius.

Underneath:
- **Caves** everywhere, as in the overworld, without dripstone spikes (the cavern biome drops large and pointed dripstone).
- **The giant cavern**, under the summit (`GIANT_CAVERN`): not a dug shape but natural caves, the large pillared "cheese" caves of the Caves & Cliffs update grown into a cave system about 200 blocks across. Inside its zone the noise router adds a second set of cheese caves (vanilla's formula, on vanilla's cave noise without its two widest octaves, `noise/cavern`) with a lower threshold (`boost`) and less layering (`CAVERN_LAYER_CUT`; the giant cavern keeps half of it, `layer_cut`), fading back to plain rock towards the edge of the zone (`CAVERN_FADE`, `CAVERN_EDGE`), so its caves merge into big chambers with pillars, ledges and pockets that look different on every seed, and stay a dozen blocks under the ground like vanilla's. The giant cavern has its own noise (`GIANT_CAVERN_NOISE`, `noise/giant_cavern`: the same widest octave, finer ones weaker, about vanilla's ratios, so its walls are smooth rock with some relief rather than small lumps) and vanilla's pillars back (`overworld/caves/pillars`, max'ed in as in vanilla's final density, which the cavern's caves would otherwise carve away); the lush cavern keeps `noise/cavern`, the full layering cut and no pillars. A winding passage (`GIANT_CAVERN_TUNNEL`) starts on its east side, in a small cave room (`ROOM_LENGTH`, `ROOM_SCALE`) so it always leads somewhere, crosses the cavern, then climbs west and opens on the western slopes of the summit. It lies in the **deep cavern** biome (`deep_cavern`, Deep Dark climate without sculk), which also fills the deepest caves around it.
- **The lush cavern**, in the south (`LUSH_CAVERN`, around z 307), made the same way and as large as the giant cavern (same radius, height and boost), entirely in the lush grotto biome (underground in and around its zone, the router writes a humidity no natural value reaches, `LUSH_FORCED_HUMIDITY`, matched only by the lush grotto, so sector borders and the erosion under the rocky ring cannot cut it up; the surface above keeps its own biome), with its own winding passage opening to the east (`LUSH_CAVERN_TUNNEL`), so the lush caves can be found without digging.
- Both keep a solid roof: their caves stop under vanilla's surface guard (`sloped_cheese`), made three times as strong (`CAVERN_ROOF_STRENGTH`) since a cavern's lowered threshold beat vanilla's and, under the low south, opened the lush cavern into the sky with floating rock between the holes. The passages are not affected, so their mouths still open.
- Cobblemon's structures choose their biomes through the same habitat tags the Safari biomes are bridged into, so the bridge invites them in, and the Safari's own ruins and towers came on top. Nothing is generated in the Safari but the structures tagged `cobblemon_safari:allowed_in_safari` (`data/cobblemon_safari/tags/worldgen/structure`, extendable by a datapack: the Safari's three henges and its three low ruins, `ruin_toppled_pillars`, `ruin_shaded`, `ruin_overgrown_trial`; not the towers, `ruin_town_center` nor `ruin_frozen_altar`, too tall or too wide, nor Cobblemon's own copies of these ruins, so there is one layer of ruins): `worldgen/SafariStructureFilter`, through `mixin/ChunkGeneratorStructureStateMixin`, drops every structure set none of whose structures is allowed when the Safari's chunk generator lists its sets (once per level, nothing per chunk), and `mixin/ChunkGeneratorStructureMixin` refuses each start of a structure not allowed in a set shared with allowed ones (`ChunkGenerator.tryGenerateStructure`, a tag check per start tried, the Safari check cached per biome source), the generator then trying the set's other structures as for one that does not fit. Features are not structures and never go through that code, yet some build: Cobblemon's template features (type `cobblemon:structure`: ruins, habitats and fossil sites pasted where the placement finds room, added in code to the biomes of its habitat tags by `CobblemonPlacedFeatures.register`; the deep cavern, bridged into `#cobblemon:is_deep_dark`, got the deep crypt in one chunk out of three), vanilla's desert well and buried fossils. `worldgen/SafariFeatureFilter`, through `mixin/PlacedFeatureMixin` at the head of `PlacedFeature.placeWithBiomeCheck` (the call the chunk generator decorates with), refuses in the Safari every feature of those types (`BUILDING_FEATURE_TYPES`) not tagged `cobblemon_safari:allowed_in_safari` (placed feature tag: Cobblemon's surface fossil sites, `fossils/prehistoric_suspicious_mounds`, `lush_den`, `mud_pit`, `rooted_pits`, `frozen_pond`, `frozen_spike`, `powdered_deposit`, `submerged_spike`, `preserved_skeleton`, `vibrant_hydrothermal_vents`; not the cave ones, `dripstone_oasis`, `enhydro_agate`, `mossy_pond`): a cached Safari check, then a type lookup and a tag check per building feature, at generation only. `place`, used by `/place feature` and nested features, is left alone. Terrain and plants (trees, flowers, geodes, ice spikes, huge mushrooms, ores, disks) stay. The rest houses keep no room for a ruin set the Safari does not allow (`RestHouseSites`).
- Both are written to `safari/carved_caves` (min'ed into the final density, interpolated like the terrain) and kept dry: `safari/carved_caves_dry` lowers the router's fluid floodedness in and around them, so aquifers never fill them.

The layout relies on one Java density function type, `cobblemon_safari:island_coordinate`, which exposes distance and direction from the island centre; everything else is data. Sector borders are wobbled by noise (`CLIMATE_NOISE`), so no two maps look alike and no border is a straight line, while the smallest sector of any map keeps about three quarters of its nominal size.

The sky palette (turquoise zenith, golden horizon, coral sunsets, low clouds) comes from biome colours plus `SafariSkyEffects` on the client.

Run `python3 tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py` after changing biomes, tags, island size or terrain tuning. It also writes `park/ParkGeometry.java` (park centre, border half-size, arrival point and spread, rest house anchors), so the border, the arrival and the rest houses always match the generated terrain: there is nothing to copy into a config file. It only rewrites the folders it owns; the Safari Gate's structure files are left alone.

## Biome tags (spawn contract)

Habitat tags: `safari_plains`, `safari_savanna`, `safari_desert`, `safari_badlands`, `safari_jungle`, `safari_swamp`, `safari_meadow`, `safari_forest`, `safari_deep_forest`, `safari_taiga`, `safari_snow`, `safari_ice`, `safari_rocky`, `safari_mushroom`, `safari_mountain`, `safari_volcanic`, `safari_lake`, `safari_river`, `safari_beach`, `safari_lush_cave`, `safari_deep_cave`.
Aggregates: `safari_cave`, `safari_grassland`, `safari_wooded`, `safari_water`, `safari_wetland`, `safari_arid`, `safari_frozen`, `safari_highland`, `is_safari`.

Use them in Cobblemon spawn files as `"biomes": ["#cobblemon_safari:safari_forest"]` to add Pokémon to the park only.

Each Safari biome is also bridged into the matching Cobblemon habitat tags (`data/cobblemon/tags/worldgen/biome`, generated from `COBBLEMON_TAG_BRIDGE`), so Cobblemon's natural spawns and spawn datapacks populate the park on their own. The snowfield also takes Cobblemon's snowy forest and snowy taiga tags, and the glacier its frozen ocean tag: with only `is_snowy` and `is_tundra`, the snowfield would have about six species one can meet without bait (Swinub, Stantler, Sentret and Cetoddle only come to a Poké Snack in Cobblemon). The volcano also takes Cobblemon's Nether sub-tags (`nether/is_basalt`, `is_wasteland`, `is_mountain`, `is_soul_fire`, `is_soul_sand`), so the ashen and fiery Nether fauna lives there (Litleo, Ponyta, Litwick, Duskull...); it does not take `#minecraft:is_nether`, which would also bring nether fortresses and nether ruined portals to the island, so species spawning only through that tag (one of Gastly's spawns, for instance) stay out of the volcano. The crimson, warped, fungus, forest, frozen and overgrowth Nether tags are left out too. The deep cavern takes `is_deep_dark`, Cobblemon's Deep Dark tag. The volcano also takes the badlands tag, the mushroom grove the magical one and the flower meadow the plains one, so every surface habitat has 30 to 130 species one can meet without bait. Catch-all tags such as `#cobblemon:is_overworld` are left out of surface habitats on purpose so habitats stay distinct; the underground cavern alone takes `is_overworld`, because Cobblemon spawns its cave Pokémon (Zubat, Geodude, Onix, Gible... about 126 species) through it with underground conditions, exactly as in any cave of a normal Cobblemon world. Caves are not surface habitats (`SafariBiomes.SURFACE_HABITATS`), so this changes nothing for the specials, the shop or the missions. Two sections of the config filter what comes in, one per way in (`spawn/SpawnSource`): `spawns` for Cobblemon's natural spawns, `specials` for the specials (the board's draw and the mod's spawner). Both inherit the same rules (`SafariConfig.SpeciesRulesSection`): legendary, mythical, ultra beast and paradox Pokémon each have a section (`legendary`, `mythical`, `ultraBeast`, `paradox`) with `allowSpawn` (refused by default, except paradox Pokémon, allowed) and `maxSpawnPerPokemon`, the most Pokémon of each of their species coming in that way per rotation of the specials (1 by default, 0 for no cap, which is the paradox default), plus `excludedSpecies`. Each way counts apart, so caps of 1 in both allow one natural Rayquaza and one special Rayquaza per rotation. A slot is held by each one caught in the Safari during the rotation and by each one still roaming; one that leaves uncaught (despawned, fled, knocked out) gives its slot back within about ten seconds, so a missed Rayquaza can come back for another player (`spawn/SpecialSpawnCounter`, which sweeps the roaming ones every 5 s; saved in `data/cobblemon_safari_special_spawns.dat`, format 5 (the swarm's, storm's and aurora's catches counted apart, as `caught_swarm`, `caught_storm`, `caught_aurora`), which also keeps each roaming Pokémon's way and Pokémon UUID so its capture is charged to the right count). A same-species cap keeps one Pokémon from flooding a habitat. A config at version 1, where `spawns` governed both ways, is converted on load by copying its rules to `specials`.

Refusing a natural spawn in Cobblemon's spawn event (`spawn/NativeSpawnRules`) comes too late: Cobblemon has already picked the species by weight, so a refused species with a huge weight (a datapack spawning Mew as common with weight 999999) would win nearly every attempt and leave the park empty. `spawn/SafariSpawnFilter`, a spawning influence put on each player's spawner while they are in the Safari, answers Cobblemon's `affectSpawnable` while it lists the candidates, and takes out the species refused by `spawns`, those at their natural cap and those already `maxSameSpeciesNearby` times around the player; they also vanish from `/checkspawn` there. The forbidden set is rebuilt when the config, the datapacks or the species list change; the capped and crowded sets are snapshots refreshed once a second (crowding is counted around the player rather than around each candidate spot, which would cost one entity query per spot). The spawn event filter stays behind it, for the second the snapshots lag, for a spot more crowded than the player's surroundings, and for spawners that do not carry the influence (Poké Snacks, other mods).

## Special Pokémon of the day

Every rotation (4 h by default, `specials.rotationMinutes`, aligned on midnight UTC: 0, 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 h UTC, until `/safari rotate` restarts the schedule), the mod draws a few uncommon, rare and ultra-rare species (by default 1, 2 and 3: `specials.slots.uncommon`, `rare`, `ultraRare`; `specials.slots.common` lets commons in, 0 by default, and commons never take the slots the rarer tiers leave over) from what is actually installed: Cobblemon's spawn data decides each species' habitat and rarity, and species without a natural spawn get a habitat from their types. The draw is deterministic per world and rotation, mixes rarities, and prefers different habitats. A new rotation, natural or forced with `/safari rotate`, never brings back a special of the rotation before (unless nothing else can fill a slot): the last specials drawn and the ones their draw left out are kept in the world state (`specials`, `specials_excluded`), so a restart or reload redraws the same list. Starter lines (any species whose first stage carries Cobblemon's `starter` label: Cyndaquil, Quilava and Typhlosion alike) and the Eevee line (Eevee and its eight evolutions) get a slot only one time in four between them (`FeaturedRotation.CROWDING_SLOT_CHANCE`, `SpeciesRules.isCrowdingLine`) when other species of the same rarity are available: Cobblemon files most starters as ultra-rare, where the 27 lines would otherwise take most of the slots, and the eeveelutions land together in one rarity too. The mod spawns these specials itself, near players and only in their habitat, with caps so none takes over. They keep the habits of their species: each natural spawn's hours, light, sky light and sky conditions in Cobblemon's spawn files are kept per habitat (`spawn/SpawnWindow`), and a special only appears where and when one of them is met, so a nocturnal species comes out at night only and one that wants darkness waits for it. Specials appear at the surface, so a spawn that needs to be out of the sky (a cave spawn) is read as "at night" instead (`SpawnWindow.of`); otherwise a cave dweller drawn as a special would never show up. Species without natural spawns (habitats from their types) appear at any time. Weather, moon phase and other conditions are not checked. The list is shown by the wall screens, by the operators' `/safari today` and by the NPCs; it is not announced in chat on entry.

**Swarms and full moon nights** (`events`). The swarm (`spawn/Swarm`) is drawn with each rotation from the rotation index and the world's salt (mixed with its own constant, so it does not follow the specials' draw), with `events.swarm.chance`. Candidates come from `spawn/SwarmSpawnData` (Kotlin, built at the first draw after a start or a data reload, then cached in `Swarm`): for each species, per Safari habitat, the natural spawn details a player's spawner can really satisfy there, i.e. a spawnable position type produced by one of Cobblemon's area calculators (not fishing), a condition that fits the biome without requiring a structure, and no anticondition excluding the biome. Poké Snack-only details (`isPokeSnack: true`, the only spawns of many species since Cobblemon 1.8, Gallade among them) are kept only in habitats where the species has no ordinary detail, with a reflective shallow copy of their conditions minus the snack flag (`SpeciesDetails.snackFree`; Cobblemon's own objects are never modified, so its spawner keeps refusing them); `SwarmSpawnData.satisfies` judges them by mirroring `SpawnDetail.isSatisfiedBy` with those copies. The habitat index alone was not enough: it only checks biomes, so before 1.5.0 a Poké Snack-only species could be drawn and never spawn, nor appear in `/checkspawn`. The candidates are then filtered by `spawns`' categories, `events.swarm.excludeList` (`spawn/SwarmRules`: each entry parsed by Cobblemon's `PokemonProperties`, a detail is excluded when it has the same species and every aspect and form the entry asks for, so `vulpix alolan` only removes the Alolan details) `events.swarm.rarities` (`SpeciesRules.isAllowed` with `SpawnSource.SWARM`: each legendary-class label the species carries needs `allowSpawn` there; the natural categories of `spawns` do not apply, its excluded species do) and `events.swarm.buckets` (the species' bucket in a habitat is the most common bucket of the details left there; legendary-class species always ultra-rare). The candidates are grouped by bucket; a bucket is drawn first by `events.swarm.weights` among those with candidates and a positive weight, then a species of it, then one of its habitats of that bucket; the swarm is made of its details there (`Swarm.Active.details`). Redrawn on a new rotation, an index change or a config reload. It reaches the players two ways. In Cobblemon's natural spawns, `spawn/SafariHappeningsInfluence` (a Cobblemon `SpawningInfluence` on every player's spawner in the Safari, like the novelty one) offers the swarm's details from other buckets, Poké Snack-only ones aside, among the common ones (`injectSpawns`, each detail still checked against the spot, since Cobblemon does not check injected details) and multiplies the weight of the swarm's details, by identity, by `weightMultiplier` (`affectWeight`). Since that share depends on the other species of the habitat, `spawn/SwarmSpawner` keeps a floor: every `checkEverySeconds`, for each player with a session standing in the habitat with fewer than `minNearby` wild Pokémon of the species within `NativeSpawnRules.SAME_SPECIES_RADIUS` (and never above the same-species cap), it picks a loaded surface spot of the habitat 16 to 40 blocks away and `spawn/SwarmSpawnPass` (Kotlin) runs Cobblemon's own zone generator and position resolver on an 8 × 16 × 8 zone there with the player's spawner, keeps the positions in the habitat that one of the swarm's details `isSatisfiedBy` (`SwarmSpawnData.satisfies` for snack-only ones) (every condition plus the influences' `affectSpawnable`, so the Safari's filters apply), picks one by position weight and completes its spawn action, which fires Cobblemon's spawn event (`NativeSpawnRules` judges and counts it). Only the bucket roll and the weighted pick among species are skipped. The same-species cap around players becomes `maxNearby` for the swarming species (`SafariSpawnFilter`, `NativeSpawnRules`). It is the last cell of the FEATURED page ("Swarm" where a special shows its rarity, `SafariFeaturedPayload` wire version 3, nullable `swarm` entry with its swarm habitat as only habitat, its model from the aspects of the swarm's heaviest detail). Each entry of the payload carries every habitat of the species (`HabitatEntry.allHabitats`: the main one first, then canonical order; version 3, 2 sent the main one only), shown on one scrolling line on the wall screens and in `/safari today`, joined with `board.habitat_separator`. Swarm members are a way of their own, `SpawnSource.SWARM`: the swarming species in its habitat is judged by `events.swarm.rarities` (same `SpecialCategory` shape, read by `SpeciesRules` for that source, plus `spawns.excludedSpecies`) and counted apart in `SpecialSpawnCounter` (`caught_swarm` in its save). `SafariSpawnFilter` judges a swarm detail in its habitat (`Swarm.isSwarmDetail`, by identity) on the swarm's cap instead of the natural switches and cap, `NativeSpawnRules` picks the source with `Swarm.isSwarming`. The specials and the swarm are noted in the counter even when uncapped (`recordSpawn`), so a catch is always charged to the way it came in and their Pokémon never use up natural slots; only uncapped natural spawns go unnoted, and an unnoted catch is natural and a line of `/safari today`. The "of the Swarm" mark needs the swarming species in its habitat and not of an excluded form. The full moon (`spawn/SafariHappenings`, checked once a second) is on while the Safari's moon phase is 0 and it is night by Cobblemon's spawner's hours; Pokémon of `events.fullMoon.types` then weigh `weightMultiplier` times more within their rarity (species types cached, cleared on data reload). Each player in the Safari is told once per rotation of the swarm and once per night of the full moon, with a chime. Cost: a few field reads per spawn candidate, a list of a few details per common pick in the swarm's habitat; the draw once per rotation, the swarm data once per data reload (one pass over every spawn detail); the swarm spawner, every 5 s, a biome read per player in the Safari, and for those in the habitat below the floor one entity query and one 1024-block zone scan.

**Downpour** (`events.downpour`, `spawn/Downpour`). Its life (draw when the weather starts, drawn duration counted in seconds by its own calls so a paused single player game pauses it, announcements, an optional last check before the start, the storm and aurora drawing their pools there so an event with no Pokémon to bring is not announced, the first reading after a start never counting as a start) is `spawn/WeatherEvent`'s, shared with the storm. The rain is the Safari's own weather as vanilla gives it to a dimension other than the overworld, which follows the overworld's; the mod never sets it. Once a second (`SafariHappenings.tick`), `Downpour.tick` reads `ServerLevel.isRaining()`: on a dry-to-rain change it draws `chance`, and a downpour lasts a duration drawn between `minDurationMinutes` and `maxDurationMinutes`, counted in seconds of server time, or until the rain stops or the event is switched off. The first reading after a start is not a change, so a restart cannot reroll a rain; the downpour itself is not saved. In the spawns, `SafariHappeningsInfluence.affectWeight` multiplies by `weightMultiplier` the weight of species with one of `types`, on spots where the rain falls (`Downpour.fallsOnSpot`: the spot sees the sky, as Cobblemon worked out, and its biome's `getPrecipitationAt` is rain), which reads no chunk and is safe on Cobblemon's spawn threads. The Downpour Mark checks `Level.isRainingAt` at the ball, on the server thread. Players in the Safari are told at its start (or when they come in during one) and at its end; `FeaturedBoard` adds its line to `/safari today`. Cost: a weather read and a clock read a second; per spawn candidate during a downpour, a cached type lookup, then a biome temperature read for the favoured types only.

**Storm** (`events.storm`, `spawn/Storm`). The same `WeatherEvent`, on `ServerLevel.isThundering()`. When it starts, its pool is drawn by `EventSpawns.drawPool` from `FeaturedRotation.index` (the Safari's natural species only: one of `types`, rare or ultra-rare, allowed by `SpeciesRules.isAllowed` with `SpawnSource.STORM`, which reads the natural spawns' rules (`spawns.excludedSpecies` and categories; the caps apply to the storm's own count), and not in `excludeList`, matched form by form with `SwarmRules`). Every `checkEverySeconds`, for each player with a session standing on high ground (a biome of `habitats`, or Y at least sea level plus `minAltitudeAboveSea` times the map relief's height factor, `IslandRelief.heightFactor`), with `spawnChance` and fewer than `maxNearby` storm Pokémon (entity tag `cobblemon_safari.storm`) within 64 blocks: a spot 16 to 40 blocks away, in a loaded chunk, dry ground under the open sky and on high ground too, then a species among those under their storm cap (`SpecialSpawnCounter.canSpawn`), rare three times as likely as ultra-rare (`EventSpawns.pick`); spot search and creation are `EventSpawns.spotNear` and `EventSpawns.spawn`, shared with the aurora. The Pokémon is created from the species' most common natural spawn (form kept) at a level of its natural range, through `PokemonProperties.createEntity` as the specials are, bypassing Cobblemon's spawn conditions (its species mostly live elsewhere), and noted as `SpawnSource.STORM` (counter save format 4, `caught_storm`): its capture is charged to the storm, and the Storm Mark finds it there by Pokémon UUID. A visual-only `LightningBolt` (no damage, no fire) flashes on it, at most once per `lightningCooldownSeconds` in the whole Safari. Cost: during a storm only, per attempt and player on high ground, one entity query, one heightmap read and a few block reads.

**Heatwave** (`events.heatwave`, `spawn/Heatwave`). A `WeatherEvent` with a trigger apart from its condition: the trigger is daytime by the Safari's clock (`SafariClock`: `getDayTime() mod 24000 < 12000`, not the sky's brightness, which rain darkens), so it is drawn at dawn only; the condition is daytime without rain, so it ends at nightfall or when rain comes, and rain stopping in the afternoon draws nothing. In the spawns, `SafariHappeningsInfluence.affectWeight` multiplies by `weightMultiplier` the weight of species with one of `types` when the spot's biome (`SpawnablePosition.getBiomeHolder`, read on the spawn threads without a chunk) is one of `habitats`. No spawner, no mark. Cost: a time, weather and clock read a second; per spawn candidate during a heatwave, a cached type lookup, then a biome key comparison for the favoured types only.

**Aurora** (`events.aurora`, `spawn/Aurora`). A `WeatherEvent` drawn at nightfall (`SafariClock.isNight`) and lasting through a dry night. Two groups (`Aurora.Group`), each with its pool, promise and previous gift per player: `FROZEN` draws `frozenTypes` with `EventSpawns.Weights.WITH_UNCOMMON` (uncommon, rare and ultra-rare, five, three and one: Cobblemon's data has almost no rare Ice species), `ELSEWHERE` draws `otherTypes` with `Weights.RARE_FINDS` (rare and ultra-rare, three to one), both `SpawnSource.AURORA` under the natural rules, `excludeList`. Every 3 seconds, each player with a session gets one try for the group of where they stand (frozen lands: a biome of `frozenHabitats`, or `Biome.getPrecipitationAt` is snow there, which covers cold high ground on any relief), unless already served for it: `EventSpawns.spotNear` 16 to 32 blocks away on ground of the same group, `Aurora.pickFor` (`EventSpawns.pick` with a per-player bias: 0 for the species the player got of that group at their previous aurora, kept in memory, and `spawns.caughtSpeciesWeight` for those in `SafariDex.caughtBy`; without the bias when it leaves nothing), `EventSpawns.spawn` (tag `cobblemon_safari.aurora`, noted as `SpawnSource.AURORA`, counter save format 5, `caught_aurora`), Glowing for `glowSeconds`, and a message naming the species. Served players are kept by UUID until the aurora ends, so a reconnection earns nothing more. Cobblemon clears every effect when it sets a Pokémon entity up (`PokemonServerDelegate.updateAttributes` calls `removeAllEffects`), a tick after it appears: `Aurora.keepGlowing` follows the aurora's Pokémon by entity UUID until their glow's end (game time) and puts Glowing back with the time left whenever it is gone, once a second, even after the aurora ends. The Aurora Mark finds the Pokémon by its UUID in the counter, so the natural Pokémon caught during an aurora do not get it. Shiny boost: `Aurora.register` (from `CobblemonSafari.init`) subscribes to `CobblemonEvents.SHINY_CHANCE_CALCULATION` and adds a modification function that divides Cobblemon's shiny rate ("1 in N") by `shinyChanceMultiplier` when an aurora is on and the roll's player stands in the Safari; it reads a volatile copy of the multiplier, set each second, since Cobblemon may roll on its spawn threads. A roll without a player keeps its odds. Cost: during an aurora only, every 3 s per player still owed a Pokémon, one biome read, then one heightmap read and a few block reads; per Pokémon rolled, a comparison.

**Egg nests** (`events.eggNests`, `spawn/EggNests`, `spawn/EggSpecies`, `egg/SafariNestEntity`, `egg/SafariEggItem`). A permanent spawner, ticked once a second by `SafariHappenings`, never announced. Every `checkEverySeconds`, each player with a session gets a draw at `spawnChance`; on a success, `EventSpawns.spotNear` (dry ground under the sky, feet and head in air or in a plant one walks through, `EventSpawns.isOpen`, loaded chunk, off the rest houses) with a zone test refusing feet or head that are not air (a nest never sits in grass or flowers, where it would be hidden) and any spot that has a `SafariNestEntity` within `exclusionRadius` (an entity query over that box: loaded nests only), then an `EggSpecies.draw`, then a nest entity with a random yaw. `EggSpecies` groups `PokemonSpecies.implemented` by first stage (`SpeciesRules.lineRoot`, whose first stage must be implemented) once per data reload (`SafariServerEvents.onDataReload`), and at each draw filters the lines by the config: one species of the line with egg groups none of which is in `excludedEggGroups` (per species: a baby is often Undiscovered while its adults breed), no species of a legendary-class label left off in `rarities`, first stage neither in `excludeList` nor in `spawns.excludedSpecies`; then one line uniformly, its first stage hatching. The nest (`entity/SafariEntities`, type `cobblemon_safari:safari_nest`, 0.8 × 0.5, tracked 8 chunks away, no position updates, fire-immune, no gravity) saves its species, its end as level game time and the UUIDs of its takers; its tick discards it once the game time passes its end, so a nest outlives no restart beyond its time; a nest without species or end (summoned by hand) draws them. It cannot be hurt, pushed or attacked; a right click (main hand) gives an `SafariEggItem` stack (dropped at the feet when the inventory is full), then discards the nest or notes the taker, per `firstComeFirstServed`. It is drawn by `client/egg/SafariNestRenderer` (registered by each loader: Fabric API `EntityRendererRegistry`, NeoForge `EntityRenderersEvent.RegisterRenderers`), which renders the `safari_nest` item's model (`models/item/safari_nest.json`: straw floor and rim, the egg on top) with `ItemDisplayContext.NONE`, half a block up, turned by the yaw. The egg (`safari_egg`, one per stack) keeps `species`, `hatch` and `progress` (seconds) in its custom data; `inventoryTick` adds 5 seconds (times `hatchSpeedMultiplier` when a party Pokémon has one of `hatchSpeedAbilities`, by `Ability.getName`) every 100 ticks of game time, server side, then hatches it: `PokemonProperties` with the species and level 1, `create(player)` (so `SHINY_CHANCE_CALCULATION` sees the player, the aurora boost included), `PlayerPartyStore.add` (to the PC when the party is full; with no room anywhere the egg is kept and retried a minute later), then `SafariMark.markHatched` (the Nest Mark, made the title when there is none). Its tooltip shows the minutes left from the custom data, never the species. Cost: every `checkEverySeconds`, a random number per player in the Safari, and for the few successes a spot check, an entity query and a filter over the lines (about a thousand, a few set lookups each); per nest, a comparison per tick; per egg carried, every 100 ticks a party scan and a stack update sent to its holder.

### Safari Wall Screen

`cobblemon_safari:safari_wall_screen`: a live display on a panel held off the wall by one arm; up to 3 side by side merge, with the arm in the middle. Right-click with an empty hand opens its settings (who may: `block/WallScreenSettings`): what it shows (specials, the viewer's stats, missions, the viewer's Safari Pokédex) and its tilt, a slider from -30° (facing the ceiling) to +30° (facing the floor) by 5°. The tilt is the block state `tilt` (0 to 6 steps) plus `upward` for the ceiling side: block state integers cannot be negative.

The FEATURED page shows the current specials with their animated Cobblemon models, rarity and habitat (scrolling when too long), and a progress bar: by default a viewer with a session sees their own hunting time drain on the specials and missions screens and the time until the next rotation on the record screens, anyone else sees the next rotation everywhere; each player chooses per kind of screen in client.json (`board.footers`: `specials`, `stats`, `missions`, `dex`, each `SESSION` or `ROTATION`, `config/WallScreenFooter`). Specials are split over pages when they do not fit. Everything is drawn client side from data the server pushes (`SafariFeaturedPayload`), so a screen works anywhere, in any dimension, without configuration; the picture is painted by `client/board/SafariScreenPainter`. Behind the panels, the screen shows its own pixel-art landscape (a watering hole at the foot of the volcano, `textures/gui/board_background_<light>.png`, drawn by `tools/gui_assets.py`) in the light of the time of day in game (morning, dusk or night), dimmed and cropped around the horizon; `board.backgroundImage` turns it off (plain `board.colors.background` colour) and `board.panelOpacity` (0 to 1, default 0.72) sets how much the title band, cells and bars hide it. `board.useSprites` chooses how the Pokémon are drawn: `false` (default, turning 3D Cobblemon models) or `true`, the mod's own pixel sprites, `assets/cobblemon_safari/textures/pokemon_sprites/<species>[_<form>][_shiny].png` (`client/board/PokemonSprites`), which a resource pack can override with the same names; a Pokémon without a sprite keeps its model. `python3 tools/pokemon_sprites_pack.py <folder> --mod` installs them from a folder laid out as `<dex>_<name>/<name>[_<form>][_shiny].png` or flat, `<dex>_<name>[_<form>].png` (or `<folder> <pack.zip>` makes a resource pack instead); the sprites are the E19 Cobblemon Minimap Icons, from the pack's `assets/xaerominimap/entity/icon/sprite/regular` folder, where the big Pokémon are drawn smaller so that no sprite is over 32 pixels tall (Emery319, icons by MBCMechachu, MPL 2.0: the tool writes `NOTICE.txt` next to them, which must ship with them); a species with no plain sprite gets one of its forms (`DEFAULT_FORMS`: Shellos West, Basculin Red, Deerling Spring, Vivillon Meadow, Flabébé Red, Zygarde 50%, the male of species drawn by sex...), each sprite is centred in its frame and written as an indexed PNG when it has 256 colours or fewer, pixel for pixel the same at about a quarter of the size (the 1025 base sprites: about 0.4 MiB of files). Every colour of the screen can be changed in `config/cobblemon_safari/client.json` (`board.colors` section, `#RRGGBB` or `#AARRGGBB`; a colour with its own alpha keeps it over the landscape). The wall screens' section of the client config is `board`. The rotation length is `specials.rotationMinutes` in `config/cobblemon_safari/server.json`.

## Decor

Furniture for rest houses and gates, in the mod's creative tab (`decor/SafariDecor`). Textures are drawn by `tools/decor_textures.py`, models, block states, loot tables and the axe tag by `tools/decor_assets.py`.

- **Safari Banner** (`safari_banner`, `safari_wall_banner` on a wall; one item places both, like vanilla banners): a pole, a bar and a cloth swaying like a vanilla banner's, same size, drawn by `client/banner/SafariBannerRenderer`. The cloth shows a picture chosen with right-click and an empty hand (`client/banner/BannerDesignScreen`, a grid of thumbnails with a scrollbar when they overflow, same rights as the wall screens, `decor/BannerDesigns`), saved in the block entity and sent to clients in its update packet. A picture is any texture under `textures/block/banner/`, in any namespace (32 × 64 for the shipped ones, any 1:2 size works): vanilla's block atlas takes everything under `block/`, so a resource pack adds pictures by dropping PNGs there, and a `.png.mcmeta` animates one (frames stacked vertically, `"width"` and `"height"` giving the frame size, as the animated ones shipped do: `river_waves`, `fireflies`, `waterfall`, `campfire`, `rainstorm`, `windy_grass`, `lily_pond`, `shooting_stars`, `sun_rays`, `spinning_ball` (a capture ball turning on itself), `ball_throw` (a ball thrown at an original creature, which it catches after three wobbles); a picture may pick its own frame count and frame time). Names come from `cobblemon_safari.banner_design.<namespace>.<file>`, else from the file name. Pictures are drawn by `tools/banner_textures.py`. A broken banner drops a plain banner: the picture is chosen again once placed.
- **Coat rack** (`coat_rack`): two blocks tall, a ranger's jacket on its front hook and a ranger's hat on top; a dye recolours the hat (block state `hat`, 16 colours, brown when placed; same rights as the wall screens). Built like vanilla's tall plants; only the lower half has loot, so breaking either half drops one rack.

## Rest houses

Pokémon Centers built once per map, always (they are the only place to heal inside the park), door toward the island centre. Each is the structure template `data/cobblemon_safari/structure/safari_pokecenter.nbt` (20 × 11 × 19, door on its west side as saved), placed by `structure/RestHouseBuilder`: turned so its door faces the centre, its ground layer level with the median ground under it (trees and plants looked through), on a foundation, with the volume above it and a one-block ring cleared first (trees, hill). The terrain around is reshaped over `SKIRT` (10) blocks: level with the house for `FLAT_RING` (2) blocks, then sloping back to the natural ground (smoothstep, rounded corners), cut down or filled up with each column's own top and second blocks; water is left alone and a column next to a pond is never cut below it. The template's grass and dirt layer takes the ground of the site (its most common top and second blocks: sand in the dunes, stone on the peaks, mycelium in the mushroom grove), the foundation too, and where most of the ground had snow, the open ground of the house and the reshaped ground get a snow layer; the planters keep their grass. Every tree reaching into the volume to clear is felled whole first (its logs found through its leaves, as vanilla measures leaf distance; a group running beyond the build area, or over 4,096 logs, is left), instead of being cut flat against the house; the trees the reshaping undermines and the leaves left without a log are removed afterwards, as around the gates (`gate/GateClearing`, with the house itself left out: its beams are logs). Blocks are set without reshaping their neighbours (`UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE`, and `setKnownShape` for the template): otherwise a reshaped flower, petal or tuft of grass that lost its support would break and drop as an item, Minecraft forcing the drop on neighbours whatever the flags. As a safety net, items younger than the build lying around the house are removed once it is done. Since no neighbour is updated, a fluid next to an emptied block would hang in the air until something beside it changed (`util/FluidGuard`): the reshaped ground, the plants cleared from it and the house's volume stop short of any water or lava (a block touching one is left, or filled with the ground for the volume), while the cut trees, here and around the gates, are removed the way vanilla leaves their spot (a waterlogged leaf leaves its water) and the fluids around them are scheduled to update, so they settle as when a player cuts a tree. Since no neighbour is updated, the vines of removed trees would stay hanging in the air: once the trees are gone, every vine of the area is given the shape vanilla would give it (`Block.updateFromNeighbourShapes`, top down so a vine hanging from a removed one goes too), and those left with nothing to hold are removed; this applies around the gates too. The fossil machine of the template is placed unlinked (its multiblock links were absolute positions of the world it was saved in), so it stands as decoration. Cobblemon blocks that ignore Minecraft's template rotation (TM machine, fossil machine, disc shelf) are turned by the builder itself, so they face the room whatever the house's orientation. The template uses only blocks of Minecraft 1.21.1 and Cobblemon 1.8.0: a block unknown to the running game is placed as air. There are eight at the surface, one per anchor of `park/ParkGeometry.REST_HOUSES`, generated with the terrain by `tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py` (`ENTRANCE_HOUSE_NORTH_OF_ARRIVAL`, `REST_HOUSE_RING`, `REST_HOUSE_BEARINGS`): the entrance just north of the arrival point, then seven on the arrival's ring, every 45 degrees from east (east, south and west first, then the four diagonals). They are not in the server config, so they always match the island. Each anchor is a starting point: `structure/RestHouseSites` reads the terrain the generator will produce (without generating chunks) on a grid around it and keeps the flattest dry spots inside the park (checked again for water on a 4-block grid of the whole footprint), widening the search to twice then four times its radius when nothing passes; then each spot, best first, is checked for water on the real terrain under the house and two blocks around it, and the first dry one is built on (the driest if all are wet, logged). Spots are at least 64 blocks from the other houses and clear of the ruin and tower spots; the entrance is searched within 32 blocks of its anchor first, the others within 64. Where each house went is saved in the world state. Inside, as built in the template: two Cobblemon Healing Machines behind the counter that never run out of charge (saved infinite), a Cobblemon PC, a cooking corner with a Poké Pot on a Cobblemon campfire, the wall screens (the specials, the record, the missions and the Safari Pokédex), Safari Banners (the ranger compass and the paw trail on a wall, the Safari crest on each side of the door, the tropical leaves standing in the lounge, their pictures saved in the template's block entities), a coat rack with a green hat in a corner, Cobblemon's fossil machine as decoration and vanilla bookshelves. `SafariProtection` refuses every click on a Cobblemon disc shelf in the Safari, except for exempt creative players, for templates replaced by a datapack. When: at server start every house gets its site (searches in parallel on background threads, noise only) and none is built. The start waits for none of them: `RestHouses.tick` takes them in as they finish, strictly in anchor order so each house's candidates are still checked against every house before it (a house whose candidates are all too close gets a second search knowing them, then stands on its anchor). Waiting for them held the server start about ten seconds on each new map, and each daily reset; a player entering before the entrance's search ends waits for it (`ensureEntrance`, which then builds the house on the spot); until its search ends, a house is not in `RestHouses.sites()`, so the guide does not offer it and the spawns do not keep clear of it, a few seconds at most, while the prebuilder's 30 s delay keeps the other houses from being needed earlier. Then: the entrance house is the first one `RestHousePrebuilder` loads, as soon as its site is known (seconds after the start) and whatever `restHouses.prebuild` says, and it is built in steps like the others; a player entering before it stands has it built on the spot (`RestHouses.ensureEntrance`, from `SafariTeleporter.sendIntoSafari`, finishing a background build of it already under way). Building it during the start generated its chunks on the server thread and held up the creation of a new map for several seconds. The others are built in the background shortly after the start (see Safari Guide and trips below), or the first time a player has loaded every chunk around them (`RestHouses.tick`, a look every second), whichever comes first, one at a time, in steps over a few ticks (`RestHouseBuilder.Job`: survey, foundation and clearing, terrain in slices of 400 columns, template, then the cut trees). Building all eight at start would mean generating some 150 chunks on the server thread, about 25 seconds before a new map could be played. A planned house's spot is kept clear of spawns like a built one, and its music zone is known to clients; if the real terrain shows water there, the next site of its list is used (the last one is built on whatever it holds). Sites still to build are saved in the world state (`house_plans`). A layout change shows after the next map reset. Spawns keep clear of the whole footprint, steps included (`RestHouses.FOOTPRINT_REACH`). Players entering the Safari (and those sent back in by a map reset) arrive on the grass in front of the entrance house's steps, on the axis of its door, facing it (`RestHouses.entranceArrival`); only when that spot is not safe ground (the house not built yet, something placed there) do they arrive at a random dry spot around the arrival point.

**Underground rest houses.** Two more houses stand in the caverns, one each (`park/ParkGeometry.CAVE_REST_HOUSES`, generated from `CAVE_REST_HOUSES` in the generator): beside the cavern's passage, where it crosses the cavern's centre column, at the passage's floor, door turned toward it, so a player walking in always finds the house. They have fixed positions (no site search) and are built like the others once their area is loaded, but in a chamber the build digs (`structure/CaveChamber`, `RestHouseBuilder.Job.startCave`): a flat plaza around the house, and in front of the door a floor across the whole band the passage may wind through (its bends plus its roughened width, `CAVE_HOUSE_PASSAGE_REACH`), following the passage's slope and blending into the plaza over `CaveChamber.RAMP_BLEND` blocks. So that it reads as a cave rather than a dug box, its outline wobbles (`EDGE_WOBBLE`), its floor is roughened by noise everywhere but on the plaza (`FLOOR_ROUGHNESS`, kept low enough that no step is over one block but rarely) and its ceiling is a rough dome; the floor is moss with patches of clay and stone and a few moss carpets and tufts of grass in the lush cavern (biome tag `safari_lush_cave`), stone with patches of tuff and andesite elsewhere, filled down to solid rock but, over a void, no deeper than a rounded underside (`CaveChamber.undersideAt`: `SHELL_DEPTH` at the wall, `BULGE_DEPTH` more toward the middle along a bowl reached `BULGE_REACH` blocks in, bumped by noise), so that from the cavern below it reads as the bottom of a rock ledge rather than a block of columns hanging straight down, dug in slices of 300 columns per tick, then lit by hidden light blocks every 7 blocks (level 12). Water and lava around it are never opened: a block of the chamber that would touch a fluid outside it stays rock, since a fluid opened without a block update hangs in the air until something next to it changes. Their anchors follow the surface ones (index `REST_HOUSES.size() + i`) and are saved in the world state by their column like the others; surface houses are planned at least 64 blocks from them. Since a house can now lie under the ground, every query about a position at a house takes its height (`RestHouses.siteAt`, `isNear`, `HouseSite.spans`): the clerk's shop, the music, the guide's "you are here", the staff's search box. The height of each house's ground layer is saved when it is built (`house_ground_y`); a surface house built before 1.4.0 has none and spans every height. Arrivals and specials, placed at the surface, only keep clear of surface houses (`isNearSurfaceHouse`). The staff of every house whose height is known (all underground houses, and every surface house built since 1.4.0) look for their floor from the house's own roof, the highest block within the template's height (`RestHouseStaff.roofOf`), not from the sky: a jungle tree left beside a house spreads its leaves over the roof, and the sky's heightmap then put the clerk on the roof. Older houses fall back on the heightmap without leaves. A staff member found more than 1.5 blocks from their spot is put back on it, which also brings down the clerks already standing on a roof. Trip tickets are centred on the middle of the chamber (`HouseSite.ticketCentre`), which reaches far in front of the door. The guide offers each underground house as its own option, named after its cavern (`cobblemon_safari.rest_house.cave.<cavern>`; functions `safari_guide_can_cave_<cavern>()` and `safari_guide_go_cave_<cavern>()`, text `{"type": "cobblemon_safari:rest_house_name", "cave": "<cavern>"}`). The Escape Rope's nearest house only includes the underground ones when the player is in a cave (`util/SafariCaves`: a Safari cave biome at the eyes and no sky above), so a player on the surface above a cavern is not sent down into it. The rules payload (wire version 3; 3 added `protection.interact`'s switches) gives each house its height span, so the music of a house underground does not play to players walking above it.

In the Safari, right-clicking a Cobblemon station (PC, Healing Machine, campfire) always works whatever the player holds, and a crouching click on a campfire is refused so the Poké Pot stays.

### Safari Guide and trips

Each rest house also has a Safari Guide (NPC class `safari_guide`, dialogue `data/cobblemon_safari/dialogues/safari_guide.json`, skin `textures/npcs/safari_guide.png`), in the south hall in front of the slab counter (`RestHouseBuilder.GUIDE_X`/`GUIDE_Z`, facing the hall), kept like the clerk by `structure/RestHouseStaff`, and removed when `restHouses.travel.guide` is off. He takes a player to any other rest house of the island. His dialogue has one option per compass direction seen from the centre of the island (`structure/Compass`), shown when a house lies there and it is not the one the player stands in (`q.player.safari_guide_can_<direction>()`, `safari_guide_go_<direction>()`); each option is named by the house's biome, through a dialogue text type of the mod (`{"type": "cobblemon_safari:rest_house_name", "direction": "north"}`, `teleport/RestHouseNameText`), with the direction added when two houses share a biome ("Jungle Safari (north)"). The biome of a house not built yet is read from the generator's noise, so naming loads no chunk.

A trip (`teleport/RestHouseTravel`, also used by the Escape Rope's rest house option) is not instant. The destination's area gets a chunk ticket (`RestHouses.AREA_TICKET_RADIUS`, 3 chunks around the house): new chunks are generated off the server thread, and a planned house builds itself as soon as its area is loaded, as it would for a player standing there. Meanwhile the player watches a cinematic (`client/travel/TravelCinematicScreen`, driven by `network/SafariTravelPayload`, wire version 2: START, SHOT, END), between two black bands with "On the way to" and the destination. It plays one of three scenes. The guide's trips are filmed (see below; not a setting since 1.4.0, which dropped the `cinematicStyle` choice); when the flight cannot be staged, they show the expedition trail of the missions screens, in the light of the time of day, slowly panning. The Escape Rope's trips are filmed too; when its flight cannot be staged, they play a pixel-art scene (`client/travel/CaveEscapeAnimation`, atlas drawn by `tools/travel_cave_escape.py`): a trainer walks out of a cave, jumps on a bird's back and flies off, then flies on above the clouds until the trip ends; the trainer and the bird are original designs. It cannot be closed with Escape, and it closes by itself if the server never answers. The player is moved once the house stands, its arrival is loaded and the cinematic has run for `restHouses.travel.cinematicSeconds` (3 s); the screen then fades out on the new place. A trip follows its house, ticket included, when a build finds the spot wet and moves to the next candidate; it is given up after 45 s, during a battle, or when the player leaves the Safari. Trips start the per-player wait `restHouses.travel.cooldownSeconds` (30 s, the guide and the rope share it, kept in memory), are refused during a battle, and the guide's only work within 6 blocks of a guide, so another dialogue cannot use the functions as a free teleport.

**Flight cinematic.** A client moves the vehicle it controls by itself, so the server cannot script the player's own mount; instead a double flies (`teleport/TravelFlight`). On the front steps of the house the player leaves (for the rope, where it was used: in the open air, the player's own spot; under cover, swimming or in the air, the best open-air spot found by `SafariTeleporter.findStandingSpotNear`, 12 blocks around at most, to which the player is brought under a black screen held for 1 s so its surroundings reach the client; no such spot, or no open sky there, keeps the pixel-art scene). When the spot cannot be filmed (trees all around block every direction of the bird, or leave no camera spot), the guide's and the rope's trips take off from the best open-air spot nearby instead, the player brought there under the black, before falling back to the painted scenes, a mount (no AI, no gravity, uncatchable, unbattleable, invulnerable) takes off carrying an NPC of class `cobblemon_safari:safari_traveller`, named like the player and wearing their skin, seated with Cobblemon's own riding pose (poser `safari_traveller`). The mount is a copy (species, form, aspects, gender, level; the party Pokémon itself is never sent out) of the first non-fainted Pokémon of the player's party that Cobblemon lets ride in the air (an AIR riding behaviour and at least one seat), or a Staraptor when the party has none. The server moves the mount along a fixed path each tick (4 s, `FLIGHT_TICKS`), toward the destination or, when roofs or trees block that path (heightmap checked at 13 points), the nearest open compass direction; no open direction gives the panorama instead. Taking off, it gathers itself on the spot (10 ticks, a flap), leaps 4.5 blocks straight up, fast off the ground (6 ticks; a gust on the ground: the wind charge's gust and burst sound, a ring of small gusts, dust and earth of the ground, a heavy flap), hangs there flapping (8 ticks), then dashes away, climbing 40 blocks away and 26 up, steeply first and flattening while it speeds up, with a rush of air (the trident's riptide sound) and a trail of small gusts for a second. Landing, it comes in from the far side, slowing down (2.5 s, the trail and the rush in the last second), brakes 4.5 blocks above the spot, flapping, its downdraught raising dust (12 ticks), turning to face the camera, drops onto the spot faster and faster (10 ticks), touches down in a smaller gust and bounces once. The client's camera stands still beside the steps (the best of 24 candidate spots: free air is required and the hall of a rest house refused; among the rest, a spot under the open sky that sees both the steps and the middle of the path, with air all around (3 by 3, two high), 2 blocks from any rest house's square and nothing at all in front of the lens (the blocks' outlines cast, so a banner, a sign or a flower counts) is preferred, then the same without those last three, then one that sees the steps, then any open-sky spot, then one under a canopy; lowest first) and turns to follow the mount (`client/travel/TravelCamera`, applied by `mixin/client/CameraTravelMixin` at the end of `Camera.setup`), with the HUD and the hand hidden (the player's F1 setting is restored afterwards). The screen goes black as the mount leaves, the trip waits for the destination as usual, then the player is moved, invisible, to its arrival; 1.5 s later, under the black, a second mount lands there with the double, filmed the same way, and the actors are removed when the player takes their place. The skin comes from the player's signed login profile, downloaded once per server run on the game's I/O pool (`teleport/TravelSkins`; Cobblemon's `loadTexture` would download on the server thread); offline-mode servers have none and the double wears the standard trainer look. The player is invulnerable during any trip; while flying they are invisible, but their armour and held items still show to others, as for any invisible player. Actors are tagged `cobblemon_safari.travel_actor`; tagged entities that belong to no flight under way (left by a crash) are removed every 5 s. At a house, the bird takes off and lands `FLIGHT_SPOT_OFFSET` (2) blocks further out than the arrival, away from the door (`SafariTeleporter.flightFeet`): on the arrival, against the steps, its wings and rider went through the roof's edge. The arriving player waits there, so the swap at the end shows nothing; when that spot is not safe ground, or the bird cannot leave from it, the arrival is used as before.

**Burrow cinematic.** A trip into or out of a cave cannot fly: when the destination is an underground house, or the trip leaves one (the guide of an underground house, the Escape Rope used in a cave, `util/SafariCaves`), it is filmed as a burrow (`teleport/TravelBurrow`), as the move Dig does. The digger is a copy of the first non-fainted Pokémon of the party whose species digs naturally (`TravelBurrow.DIGGERS`, every form: Dugtrio, Hippopotas and Hippowdon, Orthworm, Dunsparce and Dudunsparce, the Rhyhorn line, Krokorok and Krookodile, Sandslash, Drilbur and Excadrill, Onix and Steelix, Silicobra and Sandaconda, Trapinch; learning Dig is not enough, Charizard learns it) and that could learn Dig (it knows it, or its form's learnset has it by level, TM, tutor, egg, evolution or form change: `Learnset.getAllLegalMoves`); otherwise a Dugtrio. Every digger smaller than a Dugtrio at `scale_modifier` 2 (`REFERENCE_SCALE`, measured on a Dugtrio built for it, about 1.8 blocks) is grown to that height with `scale_modifier`, which grows its hitbox too; one taller by nature keeps its size, and so does Orthworm (`KEEP_SIZE`): a flat worm, it would grow some four and a half blocks wide. A digger with a seat in Cobblemon's riding data carries the double there (Cobblemon's riding controller would throw it off ten ticks in: nobody steers, the digger is never on the ground, so the controller takes it for flying and, finding no air style, ejects its passengers; `TravelBurrow.keepSeated` sets its land style again each tick, which keeps the controller from looking for a new one, and remounts the double if needed); most have none (Dugtrio, Sandslash, Excadrill...): the double then is not riding it but sits on its back in the same seated pose (the traveller's poser sits in every pose type), moved there by the server each tick, its feet `SEAT_SINK` (0.6) below the top of the digger's hitbox. In front of the house's steps (or where the rope was used, if the player stands on the ground) the digger scratches the ground on the spot (14 ticks: three small bobs, spurts of earth, the ground's hit sound every 2 ticks), jumps (16 ticks, half a block forward, 1.2 times the digger's height up, at least 1.5 blocks, lowered under a cave ceiling so the digger and its rider fit, never below 0.4) and dives into the ground, accelerating (7 ticks, ending 1.8 blocks ahead and deep enough for the double to vanish too); where it hits the ground, an impact: 50 particles of the ground at once, a puff of dust, Cobblemon's `impact.ground` sound and the ground's break sound, low. Then, until the 3 s of the shot are over (`DIG_TICKS`, which the trip's timing relies on), a rumble runs ahead under the ground along the trip: a puff of earth every 3 ticks, half a block further each time, with a low digging sound. At the destination, under the black screen, the ground first rumbles over the exit (16 ticks, growing), then the digger bursts out of it, slowing as it rises (6 ticks, the same impact), jumps onto the spot in front of the steps (16 ticks, as high) and lands with a thud and a ring of dust; coming out, the actors face the camera whatever side it found room on (it is often kept behind the arrival by the house margin below, and they used to come out with their backs to it), and the player then takes the double's place facing the house. While the actors cross the ground, earth flies up (10 particles a tick) and the ground's break sound plays every 4 ticks. The camera stands close by and low (1.2 blocks above the ground first, 4 to 6.5 blocks away, three-quarter front first), anywhere with two blocks of air that is not under a house's roof: no open sky needed. The client's camera turns to follow the digger (`client/travel/TravelCamera`), so it tilts up with the jump; the spot must see the hole, both actors' heads and the rider's head at the top of the jump, along the blocks' outlines, so a banner, a sign or a flower in front of the lens rules a spot out (the flight's test only counts blocks with a collision). A first pass also wants air all around the camera (3 by 3, two high) and keeps it 2 blocks away from any rest house's square, so it no longer films along a wall or past a pillar; only when nothing passes does the second pass drop those two rules. A player jumping or falling digs from the ground under them (`SafariTeleporter.groundNear`, 4 blocks down, else the nearest spot around); when no spot around sees the hole, the camera stands straight above it. No spot to dig from (high in the air) opens the trip on black, and the digger still comes out at the destination; no ground or no camera spot there ends the trip from black, as a flight with no open sky does. Same tags, sweep and protections as the flight; the client is unchanged (same shots, filming the digger).

**Dive cinematic.** The Escape Rope's rest house option used in the water (the player `isInWater`) starts with a swim to the nearest shore (`teleport/TravelSwim`), as the move Dive does: the double rides a copy of the first non-fainted Pokémon of the party that swims with a rider (a liquid riding style and a seat) and could learn Dive, or a Wailmer, which has both. The shore is the nearest dry spot within 32 blocks at about the water's level (3 blocks up or down, lowest first: `SafariTeleporter.findShoreNear`, which also works in caves, where the surface of the columns would be far above); in open water (the lagoon, the sea) the first land met swimming toward the destination, up to 192 blocks through loaded chunks only (`findShoreToward`). The swimmer starts where the player is: at the surface it floats half under it and dives along an arch 3 blocks deep at its middle; from the depths (the sea floor) it rises straight toward the shore, without splash. It comes up 1.5 blocks short of the bank, at 0.175 blocks a tick (5 to 11 s); with no shore in reach (the open sea, land beyond the loaded chunks) it heads 24 blocks off toward the destination and the trip goes on in black, the player left where they swam, hidden, until moved; splashes and a splash sound where it goes down and comes up, bubbles in between. The camera stands beside the start, a little above the swimmer, in air or in water (it films under water when the start is deep), seeing the start and if it can the middle of the swim. The screen fades to black as soon as the swimmer is too far from the camera to be seen (16 blocks from a camera above the water, 10 under it: `TravelSwim.outOfSight`), and the rest of the swim is skipped when the fade ends: watching empty water until the shore wasted time. Once it is over, under the black, the player is brought to the shore and the trip goes on as a rope climb: after 1 s the flight takes off from the shore, or, when the trip is a burrow (the shore is in a cave, or the destination is underground), the digger goes down there. No swimmer or no camera spot leaves the trip as before the dive. Kelp and seagrass have no collision: they block neither the camera, its sight lines nor the swim. Cobblemon's riding controller would throw the double off a swimmer that leaves the water (it has no air style): `TravelFlight.keepSeated` holds its liquid style, as it holds the diggers' land style.

So that trips rarely wait, `structure/RestHousePrebuilder` builds the houses of a new map in the background: the entrance house right after the start, always, then the others (`restHouses.prebuild`) from 30 s after the server starts; it puts the same kind of ticket on the next unbuilt house, waits for the build to end (3 minutes at most, then that house is skipped until the next restart), and moves on. It steps aside while the server is busy: when the average tick goes above 40 ms, or for 20 s after a player comes into the Safari (one more player there than a second before), the house being loaded loses its ticket and no other starts; it resumes once the average is under 30 ms, no one has come in for 20 s, and at least 10 s have passed (so a load hovering around the threshold does not drop and retake a ticket each second). A paused house starts over with a fresh time limit; its chunks already generated are kept. The entrance house is never paused. Each house built logs one line at info level, `Safari RestHouse built in Jungle 3/10` (its biome or cavern from the ids, and how many of the map's houses stand, planned and underground ones counted); its coordinates, the trees it felled, the logs and leaves of cut trees removed (the gates' clearing logs the same, at the same level), the items swept and the wet spots skipped are at debug level.

**"Loaded" means fully loaded.** A ticket makes `Level.hasChunkAt` answer true at once, while the chunk is still being read or generated; a block or heightmap read in that window (`Level.getHeight` included) makes the server thread wait for it in the middle of a tick, which with C2ME ended in a watchdog crash (issue #5, fixed in 1.3.1). Every per-tick check before reading terrain therefore goes through `util/LoadedChunks`, built on `ServerChunkCache.getChunkNow`, which only returns a chunk that has reached the full status and never waits: the house builds, `GateClearing`, `Surface.loaded` (specials' spawns), a trip's arrival and the flight's path and camera. Only rare actions still load on purpose: the entrance house when a player enters before it was built in the background, and the arrival spots of teleports (`Surface.forced`).

## Ruins and towers

The Safari's own ruins and towers (`worldgen/structure/safari_ruins/`, written by `tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py`, `SAFARI_STRUCTURES`, reusing Cobblemon's template pools) generate only when tagged `cobblemon_safari:allowed_in_safari` (see the structure filter above): the henges and low ruins are, the towers, the desert town centre and the frozen altar are not. Cobblemon's small habitats are copied the same way (`habitat_*`, each with the settings of its Cobblemon structure file and the Safari habitats its biome tag reaches), in a set of their own (`habitats`: one candidate per 256 blocks, at least 8 chunks apart, kept 3 chunks off the ruins' spots by an exclusion zone): at each spot Minecraft tries them in a random order until one suits the biome, so a map gets seven or eight, drawn at random. Cobblemon's own habitat set shares one candidate per 480 blocks between all its habitats (about four on the island) and stays refused. Every map has Cobblemon ruins and Gimmighoul towers, through the Safari's own structures (`worldgen/structure/safari_ruins/`, written by `tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py`, `SAFARI_STRUCTURES`) that reuse Cobblemon's template pools: towers in six looks (temperate, lush, rooted, deserted, sunscorched, frozen) and ruins standing above ground (henges, toppled pillars, shaded ruins, a desert town centre, a frozen altar, an overgrown trial), each limited to the habitats it suits. Two structure sets spread them: a tower candidate every 320 blocks and a ruin candidate every 224 blocks, so a map has several of each in practice. They are generated with the terrain, so they blend in like in the overworld, and change with the daily map.

## Ambience

The Safari has its own music: six original tracks composed and rendered by `tools/music/compose_safari_music.py` (pure synthesis, no samples, deterministic) into `assets/cobblemon_safari/sounds/music/`, encoded at Vorbis quality 0 (40 to 55 kbit/s stereo, 3.4 MiB for the six; quality 2 took 5 MiB, the largest part of the jar). Each biome plays the track of its zone (`HABITAT_SOUNDS` in `tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py`): `plains` (grassland, savanna, flower meadow, water), `forest` (woodland, deep forest, taiga, jungle, marsh, mushroom grove), `arid` (dunes, badlands, rocky hills, volcanic), `snow` (snowfield, glacier, peaks) and `caves`. Vanilla music is never heard in the Safari. Tracks are 1 to 3 minutes apart, and crossing into another zone lets the current track finish.

Inside a rest house the `center` theme takes over at once (`client/music/SafariMusic`, through `MinecraftMusicMixin` on `Minecraft.getSituationalMusic`); it loops while the player stays inside. The client knows where the houses are from the rules payload. Creative players hear the zone music too, instead of vanilla's creative music. A player who stays 2 s (`HABITAT_SWITCH_TICKS`) in a habitat whose track differs from the one playing gets that habitat's track as a "replace the current music" one, so the zone music follows the player instead of letting the old track run for minutes; the delay keeps a walk along a border from switching back and forth. Every change fades: vanilla's music tracks are built as `client/music/FadingMusic` (a tickable sound, whose volume the engine reads every tick; `mixin/client/MusicManagerFadeMixin` swaps it in on the write of `MusicManager.currentMusic` in `startPlaying`), which rises from silence over 2 s in the Safari, and in the Safari vanilla's hard stop of a replaced track (`SoundManager.stop` in `MusicManager.tick`) becomes a 2.5 s fade-out; vanilla starts the next track once the old one is silent. Outside the Safari tracks start at full volume and stop dead, as in vanilla. A track begun in the overworld fades too when the entrance house's theme replaces it.

The ambient sound of each biome is the cry of a Pokémon one can actually meet there (common spawns of the habitat's bridged tags in Cobblemon 1.8.0, no bait needed), a few species per biome, played quietly at random (`additions_sound`, chance 0.004 per tick, about once every 12 seconds). These `ambient.<biome>` events point at Cobblemon's own cry files.

To change the music: edit `THEMES` in the composer, run it, then run `generate_worldgen.py` (which checks every zone has its track and rewrites `sounds.json` and the biomes).

## Protection

Inside the Safari, by default: players and Pokémon, wild or sent out, take no damage (except /kill and the void; `protection.invulnerablePlayers` and `invulnerablePokemon`, judged in `SafariProtection.shouldCancelDamage` for every hit on a living entity; battle damage is Showdown's and untouched), cannot break or place blocks or use block-changing tools, fire never spreads, and explosions destroy no block. Creative and spectator players are exempt from the terrain rules. Blocks listed in `protection.placeableBlocks` (by default Cobblemon's Poké Cake and Poké Snack) can still be placed and picked up. Breaking a protected block is refused only when it would break, so mining shows its cracks and the warning appears once. Each rule can be switched off in the `protection` section of the config. Cobblemon's plants are governed one by one by `protection.interact`, through `protection/HarvestCategory`: each category names its Cobblemon block class (matched with `instanceof`, so subclasses count) and how Cobblemon harvests it. Plants picked with a click (berries, apricorns, Galarica nuts; roots with shears) have every right-click refused in `shouldBlockUseOn` when their switch is off; when it is on, the click goes on to the usual checks, so a block or tool in hand is still refused, except the plant's own harvest tool (shears for roots). Plants harvested by breaking (mints, Revival Herbs, Medicinal Leeks, Vivichokes, Hearty Grains, Bugwort, roots) skip the break refusal when their switch is on. Apricorns are also picked by a left click (`ApricornBlock.attack`), which no break event sees: `mixin/BlockAttackMixin` cancels `BlockStateBase.attack` at its head on both sides when `shouldBlockAttack` says so; any other block leaves at the first check. The switches reach the client in `SafariRulesPayload` (wire version 3) as `harvestMask`, a bit per `HarvestCategory` ordinal, so the client refuses the same clicks; categories must only ever be appended. Cost: a loop of ten `instanceof` checks per block click, break and start of a left click; nothing per tick. With `protection.hunger` on (off by default), the food bar of players inside does not go down either: `mixin/PlayerExhaustionMixin` cancels `Player.causeFoodExhaustion`, the one entry of every exhaustion source (movement, healing, attacks, the Hunger effect).

## Safari Game loop

- **Entry**: always paid with banked Safari time, which only tickets (or `/safari credit add`) provide: walk into a Safari Gate, talk to its warden, `/safari enter` (op), or any custom access method through `SafariAccess`. A ticket can carry its own duration: `/give @p cobblemon_safari:safari_ticket[custom_data={duration_seconds:600}]`. Used inside, a ticket extends the running session.
- **Poké Balls**: players bring their own (the balls listed in `balls.allowed`, Safari Balls by default, which Cobblemon crafts from apricorns). The shop gives 32 free Safari Balls once a day, theirs to keep (see the shop below). A player entering without any allowed ball is told so on entry, with the rest houses' clerk to turn to. The games' loan is still there for servers that want it, off by default (`balls.loan.count`, 0): lent balls are handed out on entry, taken back on exit, only work inside the Safari and are reclaimed from any player found outside with one.
- **HUD**: the time left only (`SafariTimerPayload`, wire version 2: the clock alone; 2 dropped the catch and loaned ball counts, which no screen showed any more), under a Safari Ball turned stopwatch (white dial, gold crown and pusher; `textures/gui/safari_timer.png`, drawn by `tools/timer_icon.py`, replaceable by a resource pack) whose red hand turns around the dial, faster and faster as time runs out (one turn in 8 s with 15 minutes or more left, about one a second at 5 minutes, two a second at the end; eight crisp positions drawn by `SafariTimerHud`, the stopwatch itself stays still), with the time under it, in the bottom left corner by default, the two lines together as high as the hotbar and level with it (`hud.anchor` BOTTOM_LEFT, `hud.offsetX/Y` 20 and -16 place the middle of the widget, 5 px above the bottom of the screen, in `config/cobblemon_safari/client.json`). In chat, the time left is given on entry, at each of `hunt.warningsAtSeconds` (15 min, 10 min, 5 min, 1 min, 30 s and 10 s by default; white, yellow from 5 minutes, red from 1 minute, by the time left), when a ticket extends the hunt, and on `/safari`.
- **Recap** at the end of each game (`session/SafariRecap`): time hunted, the Pokémon caught, the balls handed back, then, only when there is something to say, the Safari points earned and the missions completed during the hunt (tallies kept on the session, saved with it: `points_earned`, `missions_completed`, fed by `SafariStatistics` through `SafariSessionManager.recordStat`), the species new to the player with their Safari Pokédex count (`new_species`, from the statistics listener), a record beaten (more catches than the best hunt so far, read before the statistics raise it, not on the first hunts) and the Safari advancements completed since the hunt began (`started_at`, wall-clock; one pass over the server's advancements per hunt; sessions saved before 1.4.0 list none). Lifetime records: `/safari stats [player]`, `/safari top`.
- **Safari Pokédex** (`dex/SafariDex`): every species with natural spawn data in one of the Safari's surface habitats (the habitat index, `natural` entries only, so species the index only guesses from their types, most legendaries, are left out and the Pokédex can be completed), met in the wild and not only through Poké Snacks (`HabitatEntry.wildHabitats`: the habitats of its details' conditions without `isPokeSnack: true`, since 1.5.0), allowed in by the config through either way a Pokémon comes in, counted once in its main habitat among those wild habitats (`wildMainHabitat`, same rule as the board's main habitat). Rebuilt when the index changes (`FeaturedRotation.revision`, checked once a second: data reload, `/safari reload`, the late first build of the index) and then pushed to every player; also sent on join and when a player catches a species new to them (`SafariDexPayload`, counts per habitat only, wire version 1). The DEX page of the wall screens shows an overall row then one row per habitat, with bars, in as many columns as the screen's width allows (90 canvas pixels each at least), paged; `/safari dex` prints the same per habitat, and `/safari dex <habitat>` (biome path, suggested) lists the habitat's species caught and missing, by national Pokédex number. Cave-only species are not in the index, so not in the Pokédex either.
- **Safari Mark**: every Pokémon caught in the Safari gets the mark `cobblemon_safari:mark_safari` ("the Safari Find", `capture/SafariMark`, `capture.marks.safari`). It is plain Cobblemon mark data (`data/cobblemon_safari/marks/mark_safari.json`, icon `textures/gui/mark/mark_safari.png` drawn by `tools/safari_mark_icon.py`), so Cobblemon loads, saves and syncs it; its `chance` is 0, so Cobblemon never rolls it on a wild spawn. It is added at the end of the capture (lowest priority) and made the active mark only when the Pokémon has none, so a mark Cobblemon gave on the same capture keeps its place. A datapack can replace the file to change its colour or texture.
- **Event marks** (`capture/SafariMark`, `capture.marks`): on top of the Safari Mark, a catch made at a remarkable moment gets `mark_safari_star` (one of the specials of the rotation, "the Star of the Day"), `mark_safari_swarm` (the swarming species in the swarm's habitat, "of the Swarm"), `mark_safari_aurora` (a Pokémon an aurora brought, "of the Aurora", by `SpecialSpawnCounter.cameThrough` too), `mark_safari_storm` (a Pokémon a storm brought, "of the Storm", told by `SpecialSpawnCounter.cameThrough`), `mark_safari_downpour` (where a downpour falls, "of the Downpour"), `mark_safari_moon` (a full moon night, "of the Full Moon") or `mark_safari_angler` (a Pokémon fished in the Safari, recognised by `capture/FishedTracker`, "the Safari Catch"), several at once if so. Their title replaces the Safari Mark's as the active one, the rarest moment first (star, swarm, aurora, storm, downpour, moon, angler); a Cobblemon title (weather, personality) stays. `mark_safari_nest` ("of the Nest") is given at the hatch of a Safari egg instead, by `SafariMark.markHatched`, after the Pokémon is stored, and becomes its title when it has none; no Safari Mark comes with it. Same data format and `chance` 0 as the Safari Mark; icons drawn by `tools/safari_event_mark_icons.py` (32×32, the Safari Mark's frame, disc in the mark's `titleColor`).
- **Game statistics**: the Safari also has its lines in the game's Statistics screen (General tab), as custom statistics `cobblemon_safari:<name>` (`stats/SafariStatistics`, registered on both sides with their formatter): `hunts`, `time_hunted` (shown as time), `pokemon_caught`, `shiny_caught`, `specials_caught`, `best_hunt` (a record, raised at the end of each hunt), `points_earned` (captures and missions only, not shop refunds or `/safari points add`), `points_spent` (shop and mission swaps), `missions_completed`, `rest_house_trips` (Guide and Escape Rope) and `pokemon_fished` (rod bites in the Safari, caught or not). Scoreboards can read them as `minecraft.custom:cobblemon_safari.<name>`. They are saved by the game with each player's statistics (`stats/<uuid>.json`); on login, those the lifetime record also holds (hunts, time hunted, catches, shinies, best hunt) are raised to it, so a player of an older version starts with their history. Hunts and time hunted move when a hunt ends, the others as they happen.

## Advancements

The Safari tab (`data/cobblemon_safari/advancement/safari/`, 48 files with its root) and its translations are generated by `tools/advancements/generate_advancements.py`: edit the table there and run it, never the JSON (the folder is wiped on each run). Vanilla criteria cover what the game already sees: entering the dimension, standing in a biome or a structure (`minecraft:location`, checked about once a second per player until granted), placing a Poké Snack or Cake, a fossil entering the inventory inside. Everything else goes through one trigger, `cobblemon_safari:safari_event` (`advancement/SafariTrigger`, registered by each loader into the trigger registry before datapacks load): conditions `event` (an id), `value` (a vanilla int range, `{"min": 10}`) and `player` (a vanilla player predicate, read where the player stands when the event fires, which is how "fished in a cave" and "fished from a fishing boat" are written). `advancement/SafariAdvancements` fires the events: every change of a Safari statistic under the statistic's own name with the new total (so a datapack can add an advancement on any of them without code), the captures (`CatchTracker`, once the catch is recorded: shiny, special, ultra-rare special, night, Safari Ball out of battle, Master Ball, legendary or mythical, a Pokémon marked by a Poké Snack, a fished Pokémon, the hunt's catch count, the distinct species count, the last 10 seconds), fishing (Cobblemon's `BOBBER_SPAWN_POKEMON_POST` in the Safari: the `pokemon_fished` statistic, bait, shiny, special; the fished Pokémon's UUIDs are kept, 256 at most, to recognise their capture), the sessions (a new map at the start, `maps_hunted`, saved in the lifetime record from 1.4.0; a hunt of the longest allowed length that ran out), the time bank reaching its cap, a refund after an exit through a clerk, the missions (an out-of-battle one, all of them, a swap), the shop (a purchase with points, a trade) and a blow the Safari Warden or his Pokémon refused. On login every counting event is replayed with the player's totals, so thresholds passed before (or lowered by a datapack) are granted then. Whether a ball hit a Pokémon in a battle is recorded once by `capture/ThrowTracker` (from Cobblemon's catch rate event) and read by the missions and the advancements. Cost: nothing per tick beyond vanilla's location checks; a few trigger calls per capture, fish, purchase or login, each walking only that player's listeners.

## Configuration files

Everything lives in `config/cobblemon_safari/`: `server.json` (rules of the Safari, server side), `shop.json` (the shop) and `client.json` (each player's display preferences). `/safari reload` re-reads `server.json` and `shop.json`. A file that cannot be parsed (a typo, a missing brace) is never overwritten: the mod runs on the defaults and says so in the log until the file is fixed, even if those defaults would otherwise be saved after a correction (`JsonConfigFile.unreadable`).

Only what a server owner may want to tune is in the files. The park geometry (border, arrival point, rest houses) is generated with the terrain (`park/ParkGeometry`, see above), the dimension id is fixed (`cobblemon_safari:safari`), and fine-tuning values with one sensible setting are named constants next to the code that uses them (`FeaturedSpawner`: distances, radius and fallback levels of special spawns; `NativeSpawnRules.SAME_SPECIES_RADIUS`; `SafariBounties.TYPE_MIN/MAX_COUNT`; `SafariEscapeRopeItem.COOLDOWN_SECONDS`; `SafariPoints.MAX_BALANCE`). Fire spread and explosions follow `protection.protectBlocks`, and the capture bonus applies to `balls.loan.ballItem`. Unknown keys are ignored and dropped when the file is rewritten. Each file carries a `configVersion`, written by the mod: it is raised when a key changes meaning or place, so a newer version can convert older files. `server.json` is at 8 (2: the split of the species rules between `spawns` and the specials, see above; 3: the rest house travel wait moved out of `escapeRope`; 4: the mission rewards moved into the kinds; 5: the 1.3.0 ticket loot defaults; 6: the fishing missions added once to the allowed kinds; 7: the settings grouped by subject, `hunt`, `tickets`, `balls`, `battles`, `specials`, `missions`, `restHouses` and so on, with clearer names; 8: the new `events.swarm` keys, nothing converted, raised so existing files are rewritten showing them); `shop.json` is at 3 (2: the fossil ticket trade added once to older files; 3: `rotating` renamed `rotatingPokemon`) and `client.json` at 2 (2: the timer under `hud`, the footers under `board.footers`). Keys that move are moved on the file's JSON tree before Gson binds it (`config/ConfigTreeMigration`, given to `JsonConfigFile` by each manager's `migrateTree`): once bound, a key the classes no longer have would be lost. Each move only acts on a key that is there and logs it, so a file already converted, or half converted by hand, goes through unchanged; when both the old and the new key are set, the new one wins with a warning. The version-dependent steps (version 1's species rules, version 2's wait, the pre-4 rewards) are written against the layout of their time and run first, then the version 7 moves carry the result into the current layout; value conversions that need the bound classes (ticket loot defaults, fishing missions) still run after binding. Saved missions name their kind by its enum name: `BountyKind.fromSavedName` reads the former `FEATURED` as `SPECIAL`, so missions saved before 1.4.0 survive the rename (the wire format sends the ordinal, unchanged).

## Extension points

- `SafariAccess.requestEntry(...)` + `EntryRequirement`: tickets, fees, NPC gates.
- `SafariSessionManager.addListener(...)`: start, time change, catch and end events (the built-in statistics use it).
- `SafariSessionManager.addTime / setTime`: time items.

## Commands

Players have two commands: `/safari`, their time left (or that they are not hunting), banked time and Safari points, and `/safari dex [habitat]`, their Safari Pokédex (see Safari Game loop). Everything else they do goes through the gates, the NPCs and the wall screens. The NPCs' dialogues call MoLang functions of the player rather than commands (`command/SafariDialogueFunctions`, added to Cobblemon's `PlayerMoLangFunctions.custom`): `q.player.safari_shop()`, `safari_bounties()`, `safari_gate()`, `safari_leave()`, `safari_status()`, `safari_today()` and `safari_balls()` (lists in chat the Poké Balls usable in the Safari, by their item names: `balls.allowed` plus the lent ball when the loan is on; both the warden and the clerk offer it as "Which Poké Balls…?"), the Escape Rope's `safari_rope_can_climb()`, `safari_rope_climb()`, `safari_rope_can_rest_house()` and `safari_rope_rest_house()` (see Escape Rope), and the Safari Guide's `safari_guide_can_<direction>()` and `safari_guide_go_<direction>()` (see Rest houses), and the clerk's mission swap, `safari_bounty_can_swap()`, `safari_bounty_can_swap_<slot>()` and `safari_bounty_swap_<slot>()` (slot 0 to 7, see Bounties), each returning 1 when the action went through; a datapack's dialogues can use them too. A dialogue running a command in the player's name would need a command every player may type; functions are only reachable from dialogues.

Cobblemon stacks a dialogue's answers under its box with no limit, so many answers (the Guide's nine destinations at a large GUI scale) ran off the screen. In any Cobblemon dialogue, the mod's or a datapack's, answers that do not fit scroll (`client/dialogue/DialogueOptionPager`, applied by `mixin/client/ScreenDialogueOptionsMixin` after Minecraft's `Screen.init` and `rebuildWidgets`): as many as fit above the hotbar are shown (22 pixels of hotbar and a 6-pixel gap kept free, `BOTTOM_MARGIN`), with a scroll bar beside them (mouse wheel over the answers, drag the thumb, or click the track to jump a window), one whole answer at a time since Cobblemon draws its answers itself and a half-shown one could not be clipped. Nothing changes while they fit; a horizontal row of answers is left as Cobblemon lays it out. Cobblemon also centres an answer's text on its button whatever its width, so a long answer (a mission with its reward) spilled over both edges: an answer wider than its button minus 6 pixels a side is drawn by the mod instead, the button as Cobblemon draws it and the text shrunk around its centre, down to 55 % (`client/dialogue/DialogueOptionTextFit`, from `mixin/client/DialogueOptionTextFitMixin` at the head of `DialogueOptionWidget.renderWidget`); answers that fit are left to Cobblemon. Answers scrolled out of the window are parked far above the screen but stay visible: Cobblemon's answer takes a click from its last hover state alone, which Minecraft refreshes only on visible widgets, so a hidden answer kept the hover it had when it left and stole the next click (the Guide took players to the wrong house after a scroll, fixed in 1.3.0). The scroll bar, first among the screen's children, also hands a click on the answers to the one under the mouse only.

Operators (level 2) look after players: `/safari info <player>` (the same status for someone else), `/safari enter [targets]` (spends the targets' banked time), `/safari enter <targets> <seconds>` (explicit length for testing), `/safari exit <targets>`, `/safari time add|remove|set <targets> <seconds>`, `/safari credit add <targets> <seconds>`, `/safari points add <targets> <amount>`, `/safari stats <player>`, `/safari top`, `/safari today`, `/safari shop` (opens it anywhere, to check its configuration), `/safari reload`, `/safari reset`, `/safari rotate` (starts a new rotation now, with new specials, missions, shop Pokémon and purchase limits; it lasts a whole `specials.rotationMinutes` from the command: the schedule restarts there, saved in the world state as `rotation_offset` (the rotation index at the anchor, set to the next index) and `rotation_anchor_millis` (the moment of the command), so the rotation index only grows and never reuses an earlier rotation's draw or progress; the index is `rotation_offset + floor((now - anchor) / length)`, and a world never rotated by hand, or saved before 1.5.0, has anchor 0, the clock's schedule aligned on midnight UTC, on which the old offset counted, so its rotation number does not change).

## Crafting

Recipes (`data/cobblemon_safari/recipe/`), each unlocked in the recipe book once the player holds a Safari Ticket (leather for the coat rack, `advancement/recipes/`):
- **Safari Wall Screen**: 3 iron ingots on top, then glass pane, Safari Ticket, glass pane, then glass pane, redstone dust, glass pane.
- **Safari Banner**: any banner and a Safari Ticket, shapeless (a banner with patterns is used up too; the picture is chosen once placed).
- **Coat rack**: leather on top, a stick under it, three wooden slabs at the bottom.

The ticket of a recipe can be any Safari Ticket, a long one included. The Escape Rope and the ticket have no recipe: a crafted rope would be taken back as soon as its holder left the Safari, and a crafted ticket would bypass the shop and the chests.

## Safari time, tickets and capture

A Safari Ticket (one item, icon drawn from `tools/assets/safari_ticket_source.png` by `tools/item_icons.py`) adds `tickets.defaultDurationSeconds` (30 min) of Safari time: banked when used outside the park, up to `tickets.maxBankedSeconds` (4 h); used inside, added to the hunt, up to `hunt.maxDurationSeconds`. Near the cap a ticket adds only what fits, but never less than `tickets.minAddedSeconds` (20 min): with 3 h 40 banked it adds 20 min, with 3 h 45 it is refused and kept. Tickets are earned two ways. **Trades**: the shop's Tickets tab trades overworld goods for a ticket (by default 16 Apricorns, 24 Berries, 1 evolution stone or 1 fossil, any kind: a cost `item` starting with `#` is an item tag, here Cobblemon's `#cobblemon:apricorns`, `#cobblemon:berries`, `#cobblemon:evolution_stones`, `#cobblemon:fossils`, and any mix of its items counts; the card cycles through them; a player carrying several kinds of a tag is asked which one to give, in a box listing the kinds they carry (`SafariShopBuyPayload` sends the pick, wire version 2): that kind is taken first and the rest, if it is not enough, from the other kinds in the tag's order, and the trade message names what was really taken; without a pick the tag's order alone decides; a tag's name comes from the key `cobblemon_safari.tag.<namespace>.<path>`, else its id), each once per player and rotation; any shop entry can take items (`cost`: list of `item`, `count`, on top of `price`) and be limited (`limitPerRotation`), counts saved per player (`data/cobblemon_safari_shop_limits.dat`, `shop/ShopLimitStore`). **Chests**: `tickets.loot.sources` in the server config lists loot tables (a folder ending in `/` covers all its tables, e.g. `cobblemon:ruins/common/`) with a chance per chest; by default every village chest (4 %, savanna houses 10 %), desert and jungle temples (12 %) and Cobblemon ruins (18 %). Chests inside the Safari never hold tickets (its map, and its ruins, are rebuilt daily). Added by `loot/TicketLoot` through Fabric's `LootTableEvents.MODIFY` and NeoForge's `LootTableLoadEvent`. Entering at a gate spends the bank (one session at most, the rest stays banked); `/safari` shows it and `/safari credit add <players> <seconds>` (op) fills it. Leaving early through the clerk, dying inside, or being taken out by other means (a `/tp`, a home or waystone command: `SessionEndReason.LEFT`) returns the unused time of a hunt paid with the bank (`tickets.refundUnusedTimeOnLeave`).

Out of battle, inside the Safari, a Safari Ball multiplies the species catch rate by `capture.safariBallOutOfBattleMultiplier` (3 by default), which offsets Cobblemon halving every capture made outside a battle. A failed ball out of battle may make the Pokémon run away (`capture/SafariFlee`, `capture.flee`): at Cobblemon's `POKE_BALL_CAPTURE_CALCULATED` (lowest priority, so the final result), a failed throw by a player at a wild Pokémon outside a battle in the Safari rolls the chance of its entry in `capture.flee.rarities` plus `perFailedBall` per ball that already failed on it (counted by Pokémon UUID, 512 remembered at most), capped by `maxChance`. The entry is that of its legendary-class categories when the species carries a legendary, mythical, ultra beast or paradox label (none if one of them is disabled, the higher chance otherwise), else that of its rarity in the Safari (habitat index, common when unknown); a disabled entry, or a shiny while `shinies` is off, means no roll. Entries are `FleeRarity` objects with boxed fields, so a key left out of a hand-written entry reads as null and `validateFlee` puts that entry's default back rather than Gson's false/0. The flat keys of earlier 1.4.0 builds (`commonChance`... `ultraRareChance`, `specialCategories`, the switch-only `rarity`) are kept as nullable legacy fields, moved into `rarities` by `SafariConfigManager.migrateFlee` whatever the config version, then nulled (Gson skips nulls, so they are not written back). A flight waits for the Pokémon to be out of the ball and free (Cobblemon's busy lock gone), then 1 s, and is dropped if it was caught, entered a battle or 10 s passed; the Pokémon then vanishes in a puff (`discard`, POOF particles and a hiss) and the thrower reads "The wild X fled!". A fled special gives its slot of the per-rotation cap back at the next sweep of `SpecialSpawnCounter`, like a despawned one. Cost: one roll per failed ball, and one entity lookup per pending flight per tick for a few seconds. Cobblemon forces a battle with every Pokémon a rod pulls out, one second after the bite; with `battles.fishingStartsBattle` off, `battle/FishingBattleGuard` remembers the Pokémon fished in the Safari (by UUID, with the tick of the bite) and cancels at `BATTLE_STARTED_PRE` the first battle one of them enters within 2 s, which is that forced one; a battle the player starts later is left alone. Cancelled there, the battle leaves nothing half started and shows no message. Cost: nothing on battles while no fished Pokémon awaits; the map holds the bites of the last 2 s only.

While a player is in the Safari, Cobblemon's spawner around them runs `spawns.spawnRateMultiplier` times faster and allows `spawns.pokemonPerChunkMultiplier` times more Pokémon per chunk (2 and 2 by default). It also favours what the player has not caught yet: species they already caught in the Safari spawn with `spawns.caughtSpeciesWeight` (0.3 by default; lower values make caught species feel gone) of their weight, so a new species is more than three times as likely as a caught one of the same weight (`spawn/NoveltyInfluence`, a Cobblemon `SpawningInfluence` put on the player's spawner by `SafariSpawnBoost`, fed with the Safari statistics). It acts within Cobblemon's rarity buckets: caught species still appear, and a habitat whose species are all caught looks the same as with the setting off. 1 turns it off. Poké Snacks need room in that crowd (`spawn/SnackBubble`): a snack's spawner gives up when the area holds `pokeSnackPokemonPerChunk` Pokémon per chunk (Cobblemon, 2), which the boosted density already reaches, so snacks nearly never brought a Pokémon, and so were never bitten. Once a second, the snacks in the loaded chunks around the players in the Safari (as far as Cobblemon puts spawn zones, plus the clearing radius) are listed; natural spawns are refused within `spawns.snackClearRadius` blocks of one (16 by default, 0 to 32, 0 turns the clearing off; `SafariSpawnFilter.isAllowedPosition`, players' spawners only), and its spawner's cap is raised to `spawns.snackDensityMultiplier` times the Safari's density (1.5 by default, 1 to 4), so it bites before the bubble has emptied. The list and the radius are published together, so a reload never pairs one with the other's old value. Cost: the block entities of about 11 by 11 loaded chunks per player in the Safari (13 by 13 at a radius of 32), once a second, and a distance check per candidate spot against the (usually empty) list of snacks.

## Safari Gates

Rare overworld structure (`cobblemon_safari:safari_gate`) in plains, meadows, light forests and savannas: a hand-built lodge around the shimmering pane (`structure/safari_gate.nbt`, 27 × 11 × 27: its ground is the bottom layer, as the terrain adaptation expects; it carries air up to 5 blocks over the ground, and up to one block over the highest built block of each column, and leaves the air above out, so the crowns of neighbouring trees overhanging the gate are kept); the Safari Warden stands beside the pane and his Pokémon roam the plaza. Standing Safari Banners line the way in (the Safari crest) and the pond (the lily pond). Placed with `start_height` -1 and `beard_thin`: the beard lines the surrounding terrain up with the template's bottom layer, so that layer must be the ground (foundations under it leave the build standing on a wall). Placed at the `top_layer_modification` step, after trees and plants: its own chunks' trees are generated first and then cut by that air, so none grows on the plaza. What is left of them (a crown with no trunk) is removed once, in game, by `gate/GateClearing` as soon as the whole area around the gate is loaded (tried every half second from the gate's corner block, without the residents' 10 s warm-up, so a player arriving sees the cut crowns for a moment at most) (read a few columns per tick, at most 4096 blocks a tick, each column only up to its heightmap top, so a few ticks in plains and about 15 in a dense forest): groups of logs resting on nothing solid, then natural leaves more than 6 leaves from any log (vanilla's decay rule, without drops). The pane blocks of the template carry `clear_trees:1b` for that, cleared once done; a gate placed by hand never has it. `structure_clearance` (4 chunks) refuses a spot where any other structure set of the world, vanilla or modded, may start a structure within 4 chunks (its placement grid, frequency and biome; `DryJigsawStructure.nearOtherStructure`). `/locate structure cobblemon_safari:safari_gate` finds the nearest one. It only generates on dry, fairly even ground: the structure uses the mod's `cobblemon_safari:dry_jigsaw` type (`worldgen/DryJigsawStructure`), a jigsaw structure that reads the terrain from the noise around its centre (`footprint_radius`) and skips spots with water or more than `max_relief` blocks of relief. Lava lakes, features placed after the spot is chosen and invisible in the noise, are kept out by `worldgen/LakeGuard` through `mixin/LakeFeatureMixin` (head of `LakeFeature.place`): a lake is refused when one of the up to four chunks its 16-block box covers holds a reference to a structure tagged `cobblemon_safari:keeps_lakes_away` (the gate and `#cobblemon_safari:safari_ruins`, the generated list of the Safari's ruins and towers). Cost: a reference lookup per lake tried, at generation only. The Safari's ruins and towers use it too.

- **Entry**: works like a nether portal (vanilla `Portal` mechanism, same delay game rules, amber overlay; the pane is the amber dust texture slowly turning on itself, drawn by `tools/portal_textures.py`, with the spore blossom's floating spores and falling petals around it). A gate makes no sound: no hum near it, and the nether portal's whoosh is muted when a player steps in (`LocalPlayerPortalSoundMixin`; other portals keep theirs): when the countdown completes, banked time is spent and the player goes in; without time nothing happens but a message on the action bar. The player is moved a step out of the pane just before entering, so the return point is not inside it. The warden's dialogue does the same through `q.player.safari_gate()`, which only works within 10 blocks of a gate. Turn entry off with `gate.allowEntry`.
- **Warden**: a Cobblemon NPC (`data/cobblemon_safari/npcs/safari_warden.json`, dialogue `data/cobblemon_safari/dialogues/safari_warden.json`, skin `assets/cobblemon_safari/textures/npcs/safari_warden.png`, source `tools/assets/safari_warden_skin.png`). Only generated gates have one: every pane block of `structure/safari_gate.nbt` carries `keeps_warden:1b` in its block entity, while a pane placed by hand saves `keeps_warden:0b` (a gate block saved without the flag, as in a structure file, counts as flagged). He stands 2 blocks along the pane and 3 in front of it (first free of the four quadrants), wanders up to 4 blocks from there (a vanilla restriction around his spot, which Cobblemon's wander task honours; `GateResidents.WARDEN_LEASH`), never despawns. He floats and heads back to land in water (Cobblemon's `floats` and `avoids_water` behaviours in his NPC class: a datapack overriding the class must keep them), and he is teleported to his spot when found in water, more than 6 blocks above or below it, or more than 8 blocks away. He never lingers within 2 blocks of the pane (`GateResidents.PANE_CLEARANCE`), where he would be hard to click without stepping into the portal: the gate checks it every second and walks him back to his spot. His entry and his shop accept any player within 6 blocks of him, not only those within 10 blocks of a gate block (`SafariGate.isNearGate`): talking to him is being at the gate, even when he stands at the edge of his leash. Only wardens spawned by a gate carry his tag, so a Safari Warden NPC placed by hand elsewhere does not open the way. He and his Pokémon take no damage from anything but `/kill` and the void, and whoever is removed comes back on the gate's next check (every 5 s while a player is within 48 blocks), like the rest house clerk: a generated gate always gets its warden back. `gate.spawnWarden` turns him off.
- **Roaming Pokémon**: one per entry of `gate.companions` (Cobblemon properties), within `gate.roamRadius` blocks. Uncatchable, invulnerable, battles against them are cancelled. They despawn when nobody is around and come back when a player is within 48 blocks.
- **Frequency**: `data/cobblemon_safari/worldgen/structure_set/safari_gate.json` (spacing 40 chunks, separation 16; 48 and 20 before 1.3.0). Override it in a datapack to change it, or point the biome tag `has_structure/safari_gate` to nothing to disable the structure. Only new chunks are affected.
- **Operators** can build their own gate with the creative Safari Gate block (unbreakable in survival): the roaming Pokémon settle at any gate up to 8 blocks wide; no warden comes with it. To give one a warden anyway: `/data merge block <x> <y> <z> {keeps_warden:1b}` on any of its pane blocks.
- The template is built by hand in game and saved with a structure block, its bottom layer being the ground (nothing under it; a pond is at most one block deep) and with the air above the clearing height turned to structure void; its pane blocks carry `keeps_warden:1b` and `clear_trees:1b` (added with a typed NBT edit: a gate re-saved in game has lost `clear_trees` once it ticked, put it back in the saved file with an NBT editor, on every `cobblemon_safari:safari_gate` block entity; setting it in game with `/data` would make the gate clear its surroundings within seconds, before the save). `tools/safari_gate_assets.py` writes the warden's skin (`--structure` also writes a procedurally generated lodge, overwriting the hand-built one).
- **Logging in** inside the Safari without a session sends the player out, creative and spectator included (the `hunt.exemptCreativeAndSpectator` exemption only applies to players already connected). A player who gets in another way without a session (a waystone, another mod's teleport, a command) is sent back within a second to where they last stood outside, whatever their banked time: a hunt only starts at a gate (or with an operator's `/safari enter`).
- **Creative and spectator** (`hunt.exemptCreativeAndSpectator`, `SafariSessionManager.isClockExempt`): their clock is frozen while they stay in one of those modes. `tickSessions` neither counts it down nor ends it at zero for them, and sends the timer with `running` false, so the HUD shows the paused time. A change of game mode is noticed on the next tick through the `FROZEN` set (server thread, trimmed to online players each second): the player is told, and their HUD is updated at once. With an empty bank, `SafariCredit.enter` (gate, warden, `/safari enter`) still lets them in: `SafariAccess.requestEntry` with 0 ticks, which `SafariSessionManager.start` keeps at 0 instead of the 1 s minimum, with its own welcome message. Such a session expires on the first tick after they switch to survival or adventure. Entry requirements still apply. Cost: one game mode check per session per tick, nothing else while nothing changes.
- **Spawning gamerule**: with Cobblemon's `doPokemonSpawning` set to false, the Safari spawns no Pokémon at all, specials included.

## Safari points and the shop

Every capture inside the Safari earns Safari points (`points` section: by the species' rarity in the Safari, plus a bonus for today's specials and for shinies: 10, 20, 50 or 120 points, +50 for a special, +250 for a shiny), a currency separate from Safari time. With the missions (90 to 250 points a mission by default, 200 once all are done) the defaults give about 600 points per hour of active hunting, enough for a first-stage Pokémon of the shop in about a quarter of an hour. Points always show with their logo, a gold coin glyph of the mod's font (`assets/cobblemon_safari/font/icons.json`, `points/SafariPointsIcon`), in chat, the shop and on the board.

Two shopkeepers open the shop: the Safari Warden at every Safari Gate (he holds a Safari Ball) and the Safari Clerk behind the counter of every rest house (NPC class `safari_clerk`, put back automatically when a loaded house has none, `structure/RestHouseStaff`). The clerk's greeting offers, in this order: the shop, "about the missions" (a page with the current missions and the swap), "about the events" (a page with one answer per event on in the config, each opening the clerk's explanation, built on the server from the config's chances, types and habitats by the dialogue text type `cobblemon_safari:event_info`, `spawn/EventDialogueTexts`, followed by a sentence about the event's mark when that mark is on; the answers are shown by `q.player.safari_event_on_<event>()`), the allowed Poké Balls, leaving, and goodbye. The clerk lists the current missions and, after a confirmation, lets a hunter leave the Safari early (`q.player.safari_leave()`), which also walks out a player standing in the Safari without a game (a creative or spectator player exempt from the rules), to the world spawn. Their dialogue opens a prize-counter screen over a pixel-art safari landscape that follows the time of day in game: morning, dusk or night (`textures/gui/shop_background_<scene>.png`, drawn by `tools/gui_assets.py`), each with a trainer and original creatures of the mod: tabs (by default Pokémon, Items, Poké Balls, Cooking and Tickets), then cards with gently swaying animated Cobblemon models or enlarged items, 8 per page; a name too long for its card scrolls back and forth.

`config/cobblemon_safari/shop.json` (format 4) is laid out by tab (`config/SafariShopConfig`): each tab (`id`, optional `name` shown as is, `icon` item id; built-in ids `pokemon`, `items`, `balls`, `cooking`, `tickets` have translated names) holds its `fixed` offers and its optional draws `rotatingPokemon`, `rotatingItems` and `rotatingSnacks`, and may split them into `sections` (`Shelf` is what a tab and a section have in common). On load the manager flattens the fixed offers into `SafariShopConfig.entries`, each with its tab and section (transient fields), and lists the shelves (`SafariShopConfigManager.shelves`) for the draws. An older file is converted on the raw tree before binding (`SafariShopConfigManager.convertToV4`): every entry goes into the `fixed` list of its tab (its `tab`, else the one its type used to suggest), `limitPerRotation` and `cooldownHours` into `limit`, the three draws into their tab, the cooking tab's items into an `ingredients` section after the default `prepared` one; `allowSpecialSpecies` sets the legendary, mythical and ultra beast switches, never paradox. An offer sells one item (`item`, `count`), one Pokémon (`pokemon`, Cobblemon properties) or one ready-made dish (`snack`: a base and up to 3 seasonings), with a `price` in points, an optional item `cost` and an optional `limit` (`max` per rotation or, with `"lifetime": true`, for life; `cooldownHours`, real time); an offer without one takes `defaults.limit`. Limits are per player, saved in `data/cobblemon_safari_shop_limits.dat` (format 2: per-rotation counts, purchase times, lifetime counts), keyed by the offer's content (`SafariShop.limitKey`, unchanged for plain items so version 3 counts carry over), not its position. A dish is built by `shop/Dishes` with Cobblemon's own seasoning processors (`SeasoningProcessor.processors`), those of the dish's Poké Pot recipe by default (Poké Snack: `food_colour`, `ingredient`, `spawn_bait`; Poké Cake: `food_colour`, `ingredient`), so a bought snack baits like a cooked one. A Pokémon offer may set `"price": "auto"`: it is then priced like the drawn Pokémon of the first shelf that draws any (`config/ShopPriceAdapter`, resolved in `SafariShop.catalogue`); a legendary-class one costs its category's price. Select a card, then press Buy; the server re-checks everything (points, being at a gate or in a rest house, unchanged price, limits); a Pokémon goes to the party, or the PC when it is full. The NPCs open it at a gate or in a rest house (`/safari shop` anywhere for operators), `/safari` shows the balance, `/safari points add <players> <amount>` (op) gives points. `/safari reload` re-reads the shop file.

The screen shows a tab's own offers first, then each section in turn with no gap or title, the Poké Pot dishes (`shop/Dishes.isDish`, by item id) ahead of everything else in their tab (`SafariShopScreen.entriesOf`); the payload carries each offer's section (`SafariShopPayload`, wire version 2) for that order only. Hovering a dish shows the item's own tooltip, so it lists its seasonings and bait effects as Cobblemon describes them (`SeasoningTooltipGenerator`). When the tabs do not fit their row, they first tighten, then drop their icons, then scroll with arrows; no tab is ever hidden. After the fixed offers the shop sells, shelf by shelf, what it draws each rotation, the same for every player (deterministic from the rotation, the world's salt and the shelf). `shop/RotatingShop` draws `count` ordinary Pokémon among the species that spawn naturally in the Safari, each slot going to a Pokémon with a chance proportional to its rarity's `weight` (Efraimidis-Spirakis sampling without repeat), then up to `maxPerRotation` Pokémon of each allowed legendary-class category on top (a species in several categories needs them all allowed). Their level is the lowest they spawn at in the Safari, or drawn in a range; a `shinyChance` makes some shiny, priced up. Their price is their rarity's `price` times a multiplier for their evolution stage (`RotatingShop.STAGE_MULTIPLIERS`: ×1 first stage, ×2 second, ×3 third; a species alone in its line counts as a second stage), rounded to 50; a legendary-class one costs its category's `price`. A Pokémon the fixed offers already sell (same species and form, by Showdown id) is never drawn, nor one of `excludeList` (form by form, as the swarm's) nor, with `excludeFeatured`, one of today's specials. `shop/RotatingItems` draws each shelf's `rotatingItems` (`count` among the items its `prices` rules cover, plus `always`, minus `excluded` and what the fixed offers sell; the first rule matching an item wins) and `rotatingSnacks` (`count` dishes with `min` to `max` seasonings from the `pool`, only items Cobblemon has seasoning data for). A purchase made with a screen opened before a new draw is refused ("the shop changed"), so nobody buys something they never saw.

## Bounties (missions of the rotation)

With each rotation of the specials, `missions.count` missions are drawn (deterministically, from the rotation and the world's salt, so they survive restarts), each of a kind drawn by weight among `missions.allowedKinds` (`bounty/BountyKind`: species, type, special, habitat, any, different species, out of battle, night, fully evolved, level, hidden ability, shiny), different kinds first. They are the same for every player. Their targets come from `bounty/BountyPool`, the species whose natural spawns in the surface habitats pass `missions.speciesFilter`: it reads Cobblemon's spawn pool itself, spawn by spawn and condition by condition, because the habitat index of the specials merges a species' spawns and can no longer tell a fished, cave, nocturnal or rainy spawn from a plain one (built on demand, kept until the filter or the data changes). Conditions that require a Poké Snack are skipped, so a species met only through snacks (Gallade since Cobblemon 1.8) is never asked for. A kind that cannot be met this rotation is skipped. Out of battle is told from the catch rate event, which Cobblemon fires for every throw (the ball is remembered with whether its target was in a battle); night is Cobblemon's spawn night (12542 to 23459); the habitat is the biome where the ball lies. Each mission pays its reward in Safari points once per player, plus `missions.allMissionsBonusPoints` when all are done. The clerk lists them in chat, with the player's progress (the wall screens' BOUNTIES page shows them too, from `SafariBountiesPayload`, wire version 3; 3 dropped the all-missions bonus, which no screen showed), and swaps one for `missions.swap.cost` points, `perRotation` times per rotation: the replacement is drawn for that player alone (rotation, salt, player, slot and swap number), unlike any of their current missions and of another kind when possible, and starts from zero (dialogue texts `cobblemon_safari:bounty_mission` and `cobblemon_safari:bounty_swap_prompt`, `bounty/BountyDialogueTexts`). Progress, the species counted by a "different species" mission and the swapped missions are saved per player and per rotation (`data/cobblemon_safari_bounties.dat`, format 2): unfinished missions are lost when the rotation changes.

Fishing missions (`FISHING`) are counted at the bite rather than at the capture: Cobblemon's `BOBBER_SPAWN_POKEMON_POST` in the Safari moves them one step, whether the Pokémon is caught afterwards or not; captures never count for them. They are drawn only when the mission pool says something can be fished (`BountyPool.fishable`: a `fishing` spawn of a species `spawns` lets in fits a Safari habitat, whatever the filter's rarities and water switch, since the mission names no species). Captures and bites share one progress path (`SafariBounties.advance`).

## Escape Rope

Its texture is a 16 × 16 pixel-art coil with a Poké Ball handle, made by `tools/escape_rope_icon.py` from the sprite in `tools/assets/safari_escape_rope_source.png` (a screenshot: the script reads one colour per sprite pixel and snaps it to a small palette). Lent on entry (`escapeRope.enabled`) and taken back on exit, never worn out. With a full inventory it is dropped at the player's feet, and the entry message says so. A player who lost or dropped it takes another, free, from the clerk of any rest house: the shops inside the Safari list it in the items tab (built in memory, not in `shop.json`; last in the catalogue, so the other entries keep their purchase indices, but shown first by the screen, as the Safari Balls are in theirs), and refuse it to a player who already carries one ("Carried" on the card). The gate shops do not list it, since a rope outside the Safari is taken back within a second. Used in the Safari, it opens a small Cobblemon dialogue (`data/cobblemon_safari/dialogues/escape_rope.json`, actions in `item/EscapeRopeActions`): "Climb to the open air", shown only when the player's eyes are under cover (a cave, an overhang, a canopy), "Go to the nearest rest house", and "Put it away". Climbing does not go straight up: the surface of a column ignores leaves, so under a tree it is the top of the trunk, inside the crown, where players got stuck. `SafariTeleporter.findStandingSpotNear` searches ring by ring up to 12 blocks around the player for ground that is solid, dry, neither leaves nor a trunk top nor harmful (magma, cactus, campfires), with two blocks free of collision, liquid and harm (fire, berry bushes, powder snow, cobwebs, wither roses) above it, outside the rest houses and inside the park; the nearest one under the open sky wins, else the nearest one under cover, else the rope says there is no safe spot. From a cave (`util/SafariCaves`) with the cinematics on, the climb to that same spot is filmed as a burrow (`RestHouseTravel.climbOutOfCave`, see the burrow cinematic): the digger goes down where the player stands, facing where they look, and comes out at the open-air spot, under "On the way to: Open air" (`cobblemon_safari.travel.surface`); it is not a rest house trip, so it starts neither the shared wait nor the trips statistic, only the rope's own 5 s cooldown once out. Under a tree or an overhang, the climb stays instant. The park boundary's pull-back uses the same search, falling back to the bare column. The rest house option (`escapeRope.toRestHouse`) is a trip like the Safari Guide's (see Rest houses): to the nearest house, built or not, with the flight cinematic (taking off where the rope was used, after a climb to the open air from a cave) or its pixel-art scene, in front of its steps facing the door; it works in the open air too, and shares the guide's wait. Each teleport puts the rope on a 5 s cooldown. Nothing works during a battle or outside the Safari, and the actions check that again when chosen, since the dialogue may stay open. Without the dialogue file (removed by a datapack) the rope only climbs out. Any rope found outside the Safari is removed.

## Daily map reset

At the first server start of each new day (`reset.hour`, `reset.timeZone`), the Safari dimension folder is deleted before the levels load and regenerated with a new terrain seed (a salt mixed into the world seed for that dimension only, `ChunkMapMixin`). Rest houses are rebuilt on the new map; players who logged out inside are moved to the arrival point. Sessions, statistics and banked time are kept. State: `<world>/cobblemon_safari_map.json`. `/safari reset` (op) makes a reset due; `/safari reset force` (op) resets now. Turn it off with `reset.enabled`. At a start, the old folder is renamed (`<folder>.old-<time>`) and deleted on a background thread, and any such folder left by a stop is deleted too.

**Live reset** (`reset.mode` `LIVE`, `reset/LiveReset`, `reset/SafariLevelSwap`). Ticked at the end of every server tick, one step a second. Waiting: once a minute, `SafariMapReset.isDue()` (resets on, the map's day, kept in memory, is not today); a due reset starts at once if nobody is in the Safari, else after `maxWaitMinutes` with a countdown (`countdownSeconds`, warnings at 10 and 5 min, 2 min, 1 min, 30 s, 10 s and the last five seconds). `/safari reset force` skips the waiting. From the countdown on, `SafariSessionManager.start` refuses every entry. At the end of the countdown (or once the Safari is empty), the players inside are sent out by `SafariSessionManager.evictForReset`: their battle stopped, their session ended with `SessionEndReason.MAP_RESET`, which teleports them out and gives back the unused time of a hunt paid with credit, beyond the cap and whatever `tickets.refundUnusedTimeOnLeave` says. Then the drain: the prebuilder's ticket is released, rest house builds and searches dropped, and the rest house ticks (houses, staff, prebuilder) paused (the trips keep ticking, to end those of the players sent out and give them their camera back), so the empty old level unloads its chunks over the next seconds through the game's own unload, which saves them bit by bit off the tick's critical path; after at most 30 s (chunks kept by a `/forceload` or another mod never unload), the swap, on one tick: the loader's unload event (Fabric `ServerWorldEvents.UNLOAD`, NeoForge `LevelEvent.Unload`), the level removed from `MinecraftServer.levels` (accessor `mixin/MinecraftServerAccessor`; NeoForge's cached level array is marked dirty), `ServerLevel.close()` (saves what is left, closes the region, entity and POI files), the folder renamed aside, a new salt and today's day written (`SafariMapReset.beginNewMap`), a new `ServerLevel` built as `MinecraftServer.createLevels` builds a non-overworld level (derived level data, obfuscated seed, no custom spawner, the overworld's random sequences; the terrain seed from `ChunkMapMixin` with the new salt), put in the map, the loader's load event, then the per-map state as at a start (border, rest houses forgotten and searched for again, trips and prebuilder reset). The old folder is deleted on a background thread. If the folder cannot be renamed (a file held open), the map is kept and the level rebuilt on it. Players who logged out inside the old map are moved to the arrival when they come back. Cost: nothing but a counter per tick and a date a minute while waiting; the swap is the only work on one tick (closing a level with almost no chunk loaded, building an empty one); the deletion never runs on the server thread.

Each map has a relief (`reset.relief`, `worldgen/IslandRelief`: `FLAT`, `GENTLE`, the default since 1.4.0, `ROLLING`, the island before it, `MOUNTAINOUS`), applied by the density function `cobblemon_safari:relief` (`worldgen/ReliefFunction`) inside two of the terrain functions copied from vanilla: in `safari/offset`, the positive part of the continentalness spline (the land above the sea) is multiplied by its `height` (0.35, 0.65, 1, 1.6), the negative part (the sea floor) left as it is, so the coast does not move; in `safari/jaggedness`, the peaks' spline is multiplied by its `jaggedness` (0, 0.5, 1, 1.8). `ROLLING` gives exactly the earlier terrain. The relief is chosen when a map is created and saved with it in `cobblemon_safari_map.json` (key `relief`; a file without it, from before 1.3.0, counts as `ROLLING`), then read by the terrain for the whole life of that map: changing the config affects the next map only, otherwise the chunks generated later would not match those already there. The function's bounds are those of every relief, so vanilla's range reasoning holds whichever is current. `tools/worldgen/generate_worldgen.py` applies the same change when it copies the vanilla functions (`with_relief`). The two caverns' passages climb at fixed heights: on a `FLAT` map they reach the open air sooner, on a `MOUNTAINOUS` one they may end inside the slope.
